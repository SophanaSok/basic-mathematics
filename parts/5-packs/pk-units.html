Units and Measurement

A number alone does not say how much. Five what? This pack treats the unit as part of the answer, shows why multiplying by a fraction equal to one changes the unit and not the size, and uses that one idea to convert inches, ounces, cups and seconds, both in the US customary system and in the metric system. Then it turns to rates such as miles per hour and dollars per pound, where the units themselves tell you whether the arithmetic is set up the right way up.

A puzzle to carry through the pack

A car drives at $60$ miles per hour. About how far does it go in one second?

  • About $1$ foot
  • About $88$ feet
  • About $5{,}000$ feet

The speed is given in miles and hours, and the question is asked in feet and seconds, so one number has to pass through two conversions at once. The first section says how a single measurement changes units. (The second section converts a rate, by changing its top and its bottom separately.)

By the end of this pack you can write a measurement with its unit, build a conversion factor from a known fact and put it the right way up so the unit you are leaving cancels, convert one-step and two-step measurements in the US customary and metric systems, check an answer by asking whether a smaller unit should give a bigger number, find a unit rate, use distance equals rate times time with the units lined up, convert a rate by converting its top and its bottom, compare offers given in different units, and read the units of an answer to catch a setup that is upside down.
Before you start

This pack leans on fractions and decimals as numbers (Chapter 1), multiplying and dividing them, and nothing else from the course. You can read the pack at any point alongside it.

Three quick ones to wake up what the pack stands on. They are not scored. If one doesn't come to you, its solution says where to look.

Write $\dfrac{12}{60}$ in lowest terms.

Answer: $\dfrac15$

The largest number that divides both is $12$, and $\frac{12}{60} = \frac{1}{5}$. See §1.5. Minutes out of an hour reduce this way: $12$ minutes is $\frac15$ of an hour.

Compute $2.5 \cdot 40$.

Answer: $100$

$25 \cdot 40 = 1000$, and one decimal place in $2.5$ moves the point once: $100$. Check: $2 \cdot 40 + 0.5 \cdot 40 = 80 + 20 = 100$ ✓.

Write $\dfrac{90}{60}$ as a decimal.

Answer: $1.5$

$90 \div 60 = 1.5$. Check: $60 + 30 = 90$, and $30$ is half of $60$ ✓. This is how $90$ minutes becomes $1.5$ hours.

D.1 Converting units

A measurement is a number together with a unit: $5$ feet, $12$ ounces, $40$ miles per hour. The unit is part of the answer. The number $5$ by itself does not say whether a rope is that long or a bag is that heavy, and $5$ feet and $60$ inches are the same length written two ways. Converting a measurement changes the unit and the number together so that the size stays the same.

The tool is the conversion factor. Twelve inches and one foot are the same length, so the fraction $\frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}$ equals $1$, and so does $\frac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}}$. Multiplying by $1$ changes how a quantity is written and not how much it is. Units cancel top against bottom just as numbers do: $$5\text{ ft}\cdot\frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}} = 60\text{ in}.$$

Which way up does the factor go? Argue it once. We want the unit we start with to disappear, and a unit disappears only when it stands on top in the measurement and on the bottom in the factor. So the unit you are leaving goes underneath. For feet to inches the feet go underneath, as above. Turn the factor over and you get $5\text{ ft}\cdot\frac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}}$, which leaves feet times feet per inch, a unit that means nothing here. With the rule “the unit I am leaving goes on the bottom” there is no need to remember whether to multiply or divide: the units choose.

A sanity check costs nothing. The same length measured in a smaller unit takes more of them, so converting to a smaller unit makes the number bigger ($5$ feet is $60$ inches) and converting to a larger unit makes it smaller ($60$ inches is $5$ feet). If your answer goes the wrong way, the factor is upside down.

The metric system is built on prefixes that always mean the same thing: milli is a thousandth, centi a hundredth and kilo a thousand. So $1$ kilometer is $1000$ meters, $1$ meter is $100$ centimeters, and the same prefixes work for grams and liters. A metric conversion moves the decimal point, because the factors are powers of ten.

Often there is no single fact joining two units, as with hours and seconds. Then chain two factors, each with the unit being left underneath, so that the middle unit cancels as well. Every factor is still a fraction equal to $1$, and so the whole product is too. Between the two systems you need one fact, which an exam states in the question: $1$ inch $= 2.54$ centimeters, and $1$ mile is about $1.6$ kilometers. Use it exactly like any other factor, and when the fact says “about” the answer is only about.

Example 1. A factor, turned the right way

A class lasts $150$ minutes. How many hours is that?

  1. The fact is $1$ hour $= 60$ minutes. We are leaving minutes, so minutes go on the bottom: the factor is $\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}}$.
  2. $150\text{ min}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}} = \frac{150}{60}\text{ h} = 2.5\text{ h}$.
  3. The sanity check agrees: hours are the larger unit, so the number fell, from $150$ to $2.5$.
  4. Check the other way: $2.5\text{ h}\cdot\frac{60\text{ min}}{1\text{ h}} = 150$ min ✓. $\blacksquare$

Two hours is $120$ minutes and three hours is $180$, so $150$ minutes must lie between two and three hours, and $2.5$ does.

A hallway is $9$ feet long. How many yards is that?

Answer: $3$ yards

$9\text{ ft}\cdot\frac{1\text{ yd}}{3\text{ ft}} = 3\text{ yd}$. Check: $3$ yards of $3$ feet each is $9$ feet ✓.

A bag of rice has a mass of $2.5$ kilograms. How many grams is that?

Answer: $2500$ grams

$2.5\text{ kg}\cdot\frac{1000\text{ g}}{1\text{ kg}} = 2500\text{ g}$. Check: the decimal point moved three places to the right, and $2500\text{ g}\div 1000 = 2.5$ kg ✓.

Example 2. Two factors in a chain

A hiking trail is $2$ miles long. How many inches is that?

  1. There is no one fact for miles and inches, but $1$ mile $= 5280$ feet and $1$ foot $= 12$ inches. Go through feet.
  2. $2\text{ mi}\cdot\frac{5280\text{ ft}}{1\text{ mi}}\cdot\frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}$: the miles cancel, then the feet cancel, and inches are left.
  3. $2\cdot 5280 = 10{,}560$ feet, and $10{,}560\cdot 12 = 126{,}720$ inches.
  4. Check by a different path, through yards: $1$ mile is $1760$ yards, so $2$ miles is $3520$ yards, and $3520\cdot 36 = 126{,}720$ inches ✓. The size also fits: inches are small, so the number should be large. $\blacksquare$

Each factor on its own is a fraction equal to $1$, so the product of the two is too, and the trail is no longer or shorter than before.

A flight takes $3$ hours. How many seconds is that?

Answer: $10{,}800$ seconds

$3\text{ h}\cdot\frac{60\text{ min}}{1\text{ h}}\cdot\frac{60\text{ s}}{1\text{ min}} = 3\cdot 3600 = 10{,}800$ s. Check: $3$ hours is $180$ minutes, and $180\cdot 60 = 10{,}800$ ✓.

D.2 Rates with units

A rate is a quotient of two measurements, and it carries a unit of its own. If a car travels $150$ miles in $3$ hours, the rate is $\frac{150\text{ miles}}{3\text{ hours}} = 50$ miles per hour. Other rates are dollars per pound, words per minute and gallons per minute. The word “per” means divided by, so the unit after it goes on the bottom. The unit rate is the rate per one of the bottom unit: $50$ miles for each hour, found by dividing the top by the bottom. (Pack A, where rates began, goes further into ratios, but this pack does not need it.)

The rule for motion is $d = r\,t$, distance equals rate times time. Put the units in and it explains itself: $\frac{\text{miles}}{\text{hour}}\cdot\text{hours}$, and the hours cancel, leaving miles. The two rearrangements are $r = \frac{d}{t}$ and $t = \frac{d}{r}$. The units tell you which to use. Miles divided by miles per hour is $\text{miles}\cdot\frac{\text{hours}}{\text{miles}}$, which is hours. So a time is a distance over a rate. The same pattern holds for prices ($\frac{\text{dollars}}{\text{lb}}\cdot\text{lb}$ is dollars) and for pay.

Before you multiply, the units must line up. A speed in miles per hour times a time in minutes leaves a mixed unit, miles times minutes per hour, which is neither a distance nor a mistake you can see in the digits. Convert one so that the time is in the unit on the bottom of the rate: $40$ minutes is $\frac{40}{60}$ of an hour.

A rate has two units, so converting it takes two factors, one for the top and one for the bottom. Take $12$ kilometers per hour in meters per minute. The top changes by $\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}$, and the bottom, per hour, changes by $\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}}$, since an hour is $60$ minutes: $$12\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}\cdot\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}} = \frac{12000}{60}\,\frac{\text{m}}{\text{min}} = 200\,\frac{\text{m}}{\text{min}}.$$ The kilometers cancel and the hours cancel, which is how you know both factors are the right way up. To compare two offers or two speeds given in different units, bring both to one unit first and then compare the unit rates. Comparing $\$3.50$ for a bag with $\$5.20$ for a bigger bag tells you nothing until both are per pound, or per ounce.

Last, the units are a check you can run on your own setup. If a calculation for a speed ends in hours per mile, the fraction is upside down. If the unit of an answer is miles times hours, you multiplied where a division was needed. Write the unit with every number, let them cancel, and accept the result only when the unit that remains is the one the question asked for.

Example 3. Distance when the time is in minutes

A train moves at $72$ miles per hour for $40$ minutes. How far does it go?

  1. The rate has hours on the bottom, so put the time in hours: $40\text{ min}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}} = \frac{2}{3}\text{ h}$.
  2. $d = r\,t = 72\,\frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\text{ h} = 48$ miles. The hours cancel, and miles are what a distance should be in.
  3. Check by changing the rate instead: $72$ miles per hour is $\frac{72}{60} = 1.2$ miles per minute, and $1.2\cdot 40 = 48$ miles ✓. $\blacksquare$

In a whole hour the train would go $72$ miles, and $40$ minutes is less than an hour, so an answer below $72$ is the right size.

A faucet fills $36$ gallons in $9$ minutes. How many gallons per minute is that?

Answer: $4$ gallons per minute

$\frac{36\text{ gal}}{9\text{ min}} = 4$ gallons per minute. Check: $4\cdot 9 = 36$ ✓.

How many hours does a trip of $150$ miles take at $60$ miles per hour?

Answer: $2.5$ hours

$t = \frac{150\text{ mi}}{60\text{ mi/h}} = 2.5$ h. Check: $60\cdot 2.5 = 150$ miles ✓.

Example 4. Comparing two offers in different units

Store A sells $6$ pounds of rice for $\$8.70$. Store B sells $40$ ounces of the same rice for $\$3.50$. Which is cheaper per pound, and by how much?

  1. The offers are in different units, so bring both to dollars per pound. Store A: $\frac{\$8.70}{6\text{ lb}} = \$1.45$ per pound.
  2. Store B first needs its ounces in pounds: $40\text{ oz}\cdot\frac{1\text{ lb}}{16\text{ oz}} = 2.5\text{ lb}$. Then $\frac{\$3.50}{2.5\text{ lb}} = \$1.40$ per pound.
  3. Store B is cheaper by $\$1.45 - \$1.40 = \$0.05$ per pound.
  4. Check with one amount for both. At Store A, $2.5$ lb would cost $2.5\cdot 1.45 = \$3.625$, more than Store B's $\$3.50$ for the same $2.5$ lb ✓. $\blacksquare$

The comparison is fair only because both prices are for one pound (lb). Comparing the totals, $\$8.70$ and $\$3.50$, would have said nothing.

A walker covers $6$ kilometers per hour. How many meters per hour is that?

Answer: $6000$ meters per hour

$6\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}\cdot\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} = 6000$ m per hour. Check: $6000$ m is $6$ km, so the walker still covers $6$ km each hour ✓.

Practice

Give each answer as a number in the unit the question names, without writing the unit. Use a decimal, such as $4.5$, where the answer is not whole. Where a question states a fact joining two systems, use it exactly as stated. Money is in dollars.

A banner is $84$ inches long. How many feet long is it?

Answer: $7$ feet

$84\text{ in}\cdot\frac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}} = 7$ ft. Check: $7$ feet of $12$ inches each is $7\cdot 12 = 84$ inches ✓.

A bag of flour weighs $3.5$ pounds. How many ounces is that?

Answer: $56$ ounces

$3.5\text{ lb}\cdot\frac{16\text{ oz}}{1\text{ lb}} = 56$ oz. Check: $56\div 16 = 3.5$ lb ✓.

A trail is $4500$ meters long. How many kilometers long is it?

Answer: $4.5$ kilometers

$4500\text{ m}\cdot\frac{1\text{ km}}{1000\text{ m}} = 4.5$ km. Check: $4.5\cdot 1000 = 4500$ meters ✓.

A bottle holds $750$ milliliters. How many liters does it hold?

Answer: $0.75$ liters

$750\text{ mL}\cdot\frac{1\text{ L}}{1000\text{ mL}} = 0.75$ L. Check: $0.75\cdot 1000 = 750$ mL ✓.

A recipe needs $6$ quarts of broth. How many cups is that?

Answer: $24$ cups

$6\text{ qt}\cdot\frac{2\text{ pt}}{1\text{ qt}}\cdot\frac{2\text{ cups}}{1\text{ pt}} = 6\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 24$ cups. Check: one quart is $4$ cups, and $6\cdot 4 = 24$ ✓.

A film lasts $7200$ seconds. How many hours is that?

Answer: $2$ hours

$7200\text{ s}\cdot\frac{1\text{ min}}{60\text{ s}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{60\text{ min}} = \frac{7200}{3600} = 2$ h. Check: $2$ hours of $3600$ seconds each is $7200$ seconds ✓.

A phone is $6$ inches long. Use $1$ inch $= 2.54$ centimeters. How many centimeters long is the phone?

Answer: $15.24$ centimeters

$6\text{ in}\cdot\frac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\text{ in}} = 15.24$ cm. Check by an estimate: $6\cdot 2.5 = 15$, and the extra $6\cdot 0.04 = 0.24$ makes $15.24$ ✓.

$5$ pounds of potatoes cost $\$6.25$. How many dollars per pound is that?

Answer: $\$1.25$ per pound

$\frac{\$6.25}{5\text{ lb}} = \$1.25$ per pound. Check: $5\cdot 1.25 = 6.25$ ✓.

A hiker walks $14$ miles in $4$ hours. What is the speed in miles per hour?

Answer: $3.5$ miles per hour

$\frac{14\text{ mi}}{4\text{ h}} = 3.5$ miles per hour. Check: $3.5\cdot 4 = 14$ miles ✓.

A cyclist rides at $15$ miles per hour for $40$ minutes. How many miles does the cyclist ride?

Answer: $10$ miles

$40$ minutes is $\frac{40}{60} = \frac23$ hour, so $d = 15\cdot\frac23 = 10$ miles. Check: $15$ miles per hour is $\frac{15}{60} = 0.25$ mile per minute, and $0.25\cdot 40 = 10$ ✓.

A car travels $35$ miles at $28$ miles per hour. How many minutes does the trip take?

Answer: $75$ minutes

$t = \frac{35}{28} = 1.25$ hours, and $1.25\cdot 60 = 75$ minutes. Check: $28$ miles per hour is $\frac{28}{60}$ mile per minute, and $75\cdot\frac{28}{60} = 35$ miles ✓.

A bus moves at $54$ kilometers per hour. How many meters per second is that?

Answer: $15$ meters per second

$54\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}\cdot\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}} = \frac{54000}{3600} = 15$ m/s. Check: at $15$ meters per second, one hour is $15\cdot 3600 = 54{,}000$ m, which is $54$ km ✓.

Coffee costs $\$9.60$ per pound. How many dollars per ounce is that?

Answer: $\$0.60$ per ounce

$\frac{\$9.60}{1\text{ lb}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ lb}}{16\text{ oz}} = \$0.60$ per ounce. Check: $16$ ounces at $\$0.60$ each is $16\cdot 0.6 = \$9.60$ ✓.

Brand A sells $12$ ounces of peanut butter for $\$3.00$. Brand B sells $1.5$ pounds for $\$5.28$. How many cents per ounce cheaper is brand B than brand A?

Answer: $3$ cents per ounce

Brand A: $\frac{300\text{ cents}}{12\text{ oz}} = 25$ cents per ounce. Brand B: $1.5$ pounds is $1.5\cdot 16 = 24$ ounces, and $\frac{528\text{ cents}}{24\text{ oz}} = 22$ cents per ounce. The difference is $25 - 22 = 3$ cents. Check with one amount: $24$ ounces of brand A would cost $2\cdot\$3.00 = \$6.00$, which is $\$0.72$ more than brand B, and $72\div 24 = 3$ ✓.

Recap

Back to the puzzle

The car goes about $88$ feet in a second. Convert the top and the bottom of the rate: $60\,\frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}}\cdot\frac{5280\text{ ft}}{1\text{ mi}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}} = \frac{316{,}800}{3600} = 88$ feet per second. A quicker route is that $60$ miles per hour is a mile a minute, and a mile is $5280$ feet in $60$ seconds, which is $88$ feet in each. The size of a speed does not change with the unit; only the number and the unit written beside it do.

  • A measurement is a number and a unit, and the unit is part of the answer.
  • A conversion factor is a fraction equal to $1$, such as $\frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}$. Put the unit you are leaving on the bottom so that it cancels. The size stays the same.
  • A smaller unit needs a bigger number and a larger unit a smaller one. Use this to check every conversion.
  • Metric prefixes mean the same thing everywhere: milli is $\frac{1}{1000}$, centi is $\frac{1}{100}$, kilo is $1000$. Where there is no single fact, chain factors (hours to seconds, miles to inches). A fact between systems is given in the question, and “about” stays about.
  • A rate is a quotient of two measurements with a unit of its own, and the unit rate is the rate per one of the bottom unit.
  • $d = r\,t$, $r = \frac{d}{t}$, $t = \frac{d}{r}$. Line up the units before you multiply, and let them cancel.
  • Convert a rate by converting its top and its bottom separately, and compare offers by bringing both to one unit rate.
  • The unit of the result is a check on the setup: hours per mile when you wanted miles per hour means the fraction is upside down.
