Factoring and Rational Expressions

Multiplying two brackets is a routine you know, and factoring is the same routine run backward: given the product, find the brackets. This pack starts there. It reads a trinomial with a leading coefficient of one as two numbers with a product and a sum, takes a common factor out first, splits the middle term of a trinomial with a larger leading coefficient, and meets the two special forms, the difference of squares and the perfect square, each argued from expanding and each checked by expanding back. Its second section puts factoring to work on fractions whose top and bottom are polynomials. Such a fraction is undefined where its bottom is zero, it simplifies by dividing out common factors and never common terms, and a factor that is the opposite of another gives minus one.

A puzzle to carry through the pack

Try $x = 3$ in $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ and you get $\frac{8}{2} = 4$, which is the same as $x + 1 = 4$. Is the fraction $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ equal to $x + 1$?

  • Yes, for every value of $x$
  • Yes, except at one value of $x$
  • No, they only agree at some values

One test value agreeing does not settle it, and the question hides a trap in the single value of $x$ that makes the bottom of the fraction zero. The first section learns to write $x^2 - 1$ as a product of brackets, and the second uses that product to cancel, and to say exactly where the canceled fraction and the original stop agreeing.

By the end of this pack you can factor a trinomial $x^2 + bx + c$ by finding two numbers with product $c$ and sum $b$, take a common factor out first, factor $ax^2 + bx + c$ by splitting the middle term, factor a difference of squares and a perfect square, and check any factoring by expanding back. You can name the values of $x$ that a rational expression excludes, simplify it by factoring top and bottom completely and dividing out common factors, handle an opposite factor, and check a simplified form by evaluating both forms at an allowed value.
Before you start

This pack leans on solving factored equations (Chapter 4, where a product equal to zero has a zero factor) and on reducing fractions by a common factor (Chapter 1). Its polynomials are the ones of Chapter 12. You can read it at any point alongside the course.

Three quick ones to wake up what the pack stands on. They are not scored. If one doesn't come to you, its solution says where to look.

Evaluate $(x + 3)(x - 2)$ when $x = 5$.

Answer: $24$

$x + 3 = 8$ and $x - 2 = 3$, so the product is $8 \cdot 3 = 24$. Check by expanding first: $x^2 + x - 6$ at $x = 5$ is $25 + 5 - 6 = 24$ ✓.

Write $\dfrac{18}{30}$ in lowest terms.

Answer: $\frac{3}{5}$

Both $18$ and $30$ are multiples of $6$: $\frac{18}{30} = \frac{3 \cdot 6}{5 \cdot 6} = \frac{3}{5}$, because $\frac{6}{6} = 1$. Check as decimals: $18 \div 30 = 0.6$ and $3 \div 5 = 0.6$ ✓.

Solve $(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0$. Give the two values of $x$, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = -1$ and $x = 4$

The product is zero when a factor is zero: $x - 4 = 0$ gives $x = 4$ and $x + 1 = 0$ gives $x = -1$. Check: at $x = 4$ the first bracket is $0$, and at $x = -1$ the second is $0$, so the product is $0$ both times ✓.

H.1 Factoring trinomials and special forms

To factor a polynomial is to write it as a product of simpler polynomials, and the way to learn it is to look at the multiplication it undoes. Expand $(x + p)(x + q)$ by giving each term of the first bracket to the second: $$(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + qx + px + pq = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq.$$ Read from right to left, that is the whole method for $x^2 + bx + c$: it factors as $(x + p)(x + q)$ when two numbers $p$ and $q$ have product $c$ and sum $b$. List the pairs of whole numbers whose product is $c$ and pick the pair whose sum is $b$. The signs tell you where to look. If $c > 0$, then $p$ and $q$ have the same sign, and so does their sum, so both take the sign of $b$: for $x^2 - 7x + 10$ both are negative, and $-2$ and $-5$ have product $10$ and sum $-7$, giving $(x - 2)(x - 5)$. If $c < 0$, the signs are opposite, and the number with the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$: for $x^2 + 2x - 15$ the pairs with product $15$ are $1, 15$ and $3, 5$, and only $3$ and $5$ differ by $2$, so the larger one, $5$, is positive: $(x - 3)(x + 5)$.

A common factor comes out first, always. In $3x^2 + 12x$ both terms are multiples of $3x$, so $3x^2 + 12x = 3x(x + 4)$. To factor completely means to keep going until no factor can be factored again. Taking the common factor out first also shrinks the numbers you then have to search.

When the leading coefficient is more than $1$, expand $(ax + p)(x + q)$ the same way: $ax^2 + aqx + px + pq$. The middle term is two pieces, $aqx$ and $px$. Their coefficients add to $b$, and multiply to $aq \cdot p = a \cdot pq = ac$. So for $ax^2 + bx + c$ look for two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term into those two pieces, and group. For $3x^2 + 10x + 8$ the product is $ac = 24$ and the sum is $10$, which are $4$ and $6$: $3x^2 + 4x + 6x + 8 = x(3x + 4) + 2(3x + 4) = (3x + 4)(x + 2)$. The grouping works because both groups leave the same bracket; if they leave different ones, the pair was wrong.

Two forms are worth knowing on sight, and both come from expanding. In $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 + ab - ab - b^2$ the middle terms cancel, so $$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b),$$ a difference of squares: $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$. And $(a + b)^2 = (a + b)(a + b) = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, so a perfect square trinomial has its first and last terms as squares and its middle term as twice their product: $x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)^2$, since $25 = 5^2$ and $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. A sum of squares is different. $x^2 + 9$ would need two numbers with product $9$ and sum $0$; two numbers with sum $0$ are $p$ and $-p$, whose product $-p^2$ is never positive, so it can never be $9$. So $x^2 + 9$ does not factor over the real numbers.

Always check by expanding back. A factoring with the wrong signs still looks right: $(x + 2)(x - 3)$ and $(x - 2)(x + 3)$ use the same numbers, but the first expands to $x^2 - x - 6$ and the second to $x^2 + x - 6$, and only the middle term tells them apart.

Example 1. A trinomial with a negative last term

Factor $x^2 - 3x - 28$.

  1. There is no common factor. We need two numbers with product $-28$ and sum $-3$.
  2. The product is negative, so the signs are opposite, and the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$, which is negative. The pairs with product $28$ are $1, 28$ and $2, 14$ and $4, 7$, and only $4$ and $7$ differ by $3$.
  3. So the numbers are $-7$ and $4$, and $x^2 - 3x - 28 = (x - 7)(x + 4)$.
  4. Check by expanding: $(x - 7)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - 7x - 28 = x^2 - 3x - 28$ ✓. A second check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $1 - 3 - 28 = -30$, and the factors give $(-6)(5) = -30$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Had the signs been swapped, $(x + 7)(x - 4)$, the middle term would be $+3x$. The sign of $b$ decides which number is negative.

Factor $x^2 + 9x + 20$.

Answer: $(x + 4)(x + 5)$

The pairs with product $20$ are $1, 20$ and $2, 10$ and $4, 5$, and only $4 + 5 = 9$. Check by expanding: $(x + 4)(x + 5) = x^2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x^2 + 9x + 20$ ✓.

Factor $5x^2 + 17x + 6$.

Answer: $(5x + 2)(x + 3)$

Here $ac = 30$ and $b = 17$, and the numbers are $2$ and $15$. Then $5x^2 + 15x + 2x + 6 = 5x(x + 3) + 2(x + 3) = (5x + 2)(x + 3)$. Check by expanding: $5x^2 + 15x + 2x + 6 = 5x^2 + 17x + 6$ ✓.

Example 2. A common factor, then a difference of squares

Factor $12x^2 - 75$ completely.

  1. Both terms are multiples of $3$, so take it out first: $12x^2 - 75 = 3(4x^2 - 25)$.
  2. Inside the bracket, $4x^2 = (2x)^2$ and $25 = 5^2$, so it is a difference of squares with $a = 2x$ and $b = 5$: $4x^2 - 25 = (2x - 5)(2x + 5)$.
  3. So $12x^2 - 75 = 3(2x - 5)(2x + 5)$. No factor factors again ($2x - 5$ and $2x + 5$ have no common factor), so this is complete.
  4. Check by expanding: $(2x - 5)(2x + 5) = 4x^2 - 25$, and $3(4x^2 - 25) = 12x^2 - 75$ ✓. A second check at $x = 1$: $12 - 75 = -63$, and $3(-3)(7) = -63$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Stopping at $3(4x^2 - 25)$ is correct as far as it goes but not complete, since the bracket still factors.

Factor $x^2 - 64$.

Answer: $(x - 8)(x + 8)$

With $a = x$ and $b = 8$, $x^2 - 64 = (x - 8)(x + 8)$. Check by expanding: $x^2 + 8x - 8x - 64 = x^2 - 64$ ✓.

H.2 Simplifying rational expressions

A rational expression is a fraction whose top and bottom are polynomials, and it behaves like a numeric fraction. The number fraction $\frac{6}{8}$ equals $\frac{3}{4}$ because $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3 \cdot 2}{4 \cdot 2} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{2}$ and $\frac{2}{2} = 1$: a common factor divides out. The same works for polynomials, once they are factored.

One thing comes before canceling. Division by zero is undefined, so any value of $x$ that makes the bottom zero is excluded. Find the excluded values from the factored bottom, before canceling, and they stay excluded after: simplifying never makes an excluded value allowed. This is what the puzzle turns on. Factor the top and the bottom completely: $$\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1}.$$ The bottom is zero at $x = 1$, so $x = 1$ is excluded. For every other $x$ the factor $x - 1$ is not zero, and $\frac{x - 1}{x - 1} = 1$, so the fraction equals $x + 1$. The two are equal at every allowed $x$, but not at $x = 1$, where the fraction is undefined.

To simplify, then: factor top and bottom completely, name the excluded values, and divide out the common factors. Only factors cancel, never terms. It is tempting to cancel the $x$ in $\dfrac{x + 3}{x}$ and write $3$, but at $x = 1$ the fraction is $\frac{4}{1} = 4$ and not $3$. In $x + 3$ the $x$ is one term of a sum, not a factor of the whole top; the top has no common factor with $x$, so nothing cancels and the fraction is already simplified.

A subtraction can hide a common factor: $a - b = -(b - a)$, since reversing a subtraction flips the sign. So two factors that are opposites cancel to $-1$: $\dfrac{3 - x}{x - 3} = \dfrac{-(x - 3)}{x - 3} = -1$ for $x \ne 3$.

The check is to evaluate. The simplified form must give the same value as the original at every allowed $x$, so evaluate both at a number that is not excluded and compare. A different value means a slip. Equal values are good evidence, but the factoring check from H.1, expanding back, is what shows the factoring itself is right.

Example 3. Two trinomials that share a factor

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 + x - 6}$ and name the excluded values.

  1. Factor the top: product $6$, sum $5$, so $x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$.
  2. Factor the bottom: product $-6$, sum $1$, so the numbers are $3$ and $-2$ and $x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$.
  3. The bottom is zero when $x = -3$ or $x = 2$, so those two values are excluded.
  4. The common factor is $x + 3$. Dividing it out leaves $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 2}$, for $x \ne -3$ and $x \ne 2$.
  5. Check at $x = 4$, which is allowed: the original gives $\frac{16 + 20 + 6}{16 + 4 - 6} = \frac{42}{14} = 3$ and the simplified form gives $\frac{6}{2} = 3$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

The value $x = -3$ is excluded even though $x + 3$ is gone from the simplified form. The original is still undefined there.

For which values of $x$ is $\dfrac{x + 1}{x^2 - 3x - 10}$ undefined? Give the values, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = -2$ and $x = 5$

The bottom factors as $(x - 5)(x + 2)$, which is zero at $x = 5$ and $x = -2$. Check by putting each value into the bottom: $25 - 15 - 10 = 0$ and $4 + 6 - 10 = 0$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 7x + 12}{x^2 - 9}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{x - 4}{x + 3}$

The top is $(x - 3)(x - 4)$ and the bottom is $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, so dividing out $x - 3$ leaves $\frac{x - 4}{x + 3}$, for $x \ne 3$ and $x \ne -3$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{1 - 7 + 12}{1 - 9} = \frac{6}{-8} = -\frac{3}{4}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-3}{4} = -\frac{3}{4}$ ✓.

Example 4. A common factor and an opposite factor

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 25}{10 - 2x}$ and name the excluded value.

  1. The top is a difference of squares: $x^2 - 25 = (x - 5)(x + 5)$.
  2. The bottom has the common factor $2$: $10 - 2x = 2(5 - x)$. The factor $5 - x$ is the opposite of $x - 5$, so $5 - x = -(x - 5)$ and the bottom is $-2(x - 5)$.
  3. The bottom is zero at $x = 5$, so $x = 5$ is excluded.
  4. Divide out $x - 5$: $\dfrac{(x - 5)(x + 5)}{-2(x - 5)} = \dfrac{x + 5}{-2} = -\dfrac{x + 5}{2}$, for $x \ne 5$.
  5. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{1 - 25}{10 - 2} = \frac{-24}{8} = -3$, and the simplified form gives $-\frac{6}{2} = -3$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Writing the bottom with the factor $x - 5$ showing, rather than $5 - x$, is what lets the two factors be seen as the same.

Simplify $\dfrac{7 - x}{x^2 - 49}$.

Answer: $-\dfrac{1}{x + 7}$

The bottom is $(x - 7)(x + 7)$ and the top is $7 - x = -(x - 7)$, so dividing out $x - 7$ leaves $\frac{-1}{x + 7}$, for $x \ne 7$ and $x \ne -7$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{6}{-48} = -\frac{1}{8}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-1}{8}$ ✓.

Practice

Type a product of factors as (x+2)(x+3), in any order of the factors, with a number in front if there is one. Type a power as (x+3)^2. Type a fraction with its top and bottom in brackets, as in (x-3)/(x+2). Type a list of values as a set, as in 2, 5. A question that asks which option is right is answered by choosing one of the options shown.

Factor $x^2 + 3x - 18$.

Answer: $(x - 3)(x + 6)$

The pairs with product $18$ are $1, 18$ and $2, 9$ and $3, 6$, and only $3$ and $6$ differ by $3$. The larger one carries the sign of $+3$, so the numbers are $6$ and $-3$. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $1 + 3 - 18 = -14$, and the factors give $(-2)(7) = -14$ ✓.

Factor $x^2 - 11x + 24$.

Answer: $(x - 3)(x - 8)$

The pairs with product $24$ are $1, 24$ and $2, 12$ and $3, 8$ and $4, 6$, and only $3 + 8 = 11$. Both numbers are negative, since the sum is. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $1 - 11 + 24 = 14$, and the factors give $(-2)(-7) = 14$ ✓.

Factor $2x^2 + 14x + 24$ completely.

Answer: $2(x + 3)(x + 4)$

Every term is a multiple of $2$, so $2x^2 + 14x + 24 = 2(x^2 + 7x + 12)$. In the bracket, $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ and $3 + 4 = 7$, so it is $(x + 3)(x + 4)$. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $2 + 14 + 24 = 40$, and the factors give $2 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 40$ ✓.

Factor $2x^2 - 5x - 12$.

Answer: $(2x + 3)(x - 4)$

Here $ac = -24$ and $b = -5$, and the numbers are $-8$ and $3$. Then $2x^2 - 8x + 3x - 12 = 2x(x - 4) + 3(x - 4) = (2x + 3)(x - 4)$. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $2 - 5 - 12 = -15$, and the factors give $(5)(-3) = -15$ ✓.

Factor $5x^2 - 80$ completely.

Answer: $5(x - 4)(x + 4)$

Both terms are multiples of $5$: $5x^2 - 80 = 5(x^2 - 16)$, and $x^2 - 16 = (x - 4)(x + 4)$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $5 - 80 = -75$, and the factors give $5(-3)(5) = -75$ ✓.

Factor $x^2 - 14x + 49$.

Answer: $(x - 7)^2$

Here $x^2$ and $49 = 7^2$ are squares and $14 = 2 \cdot 7$, with a minus sign, so it is $(x - 7)^2$. The pair method agrees: $-7 \cdot -7 = 49$ and $-7 + (-7) = -14$. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $1 - 14 + 49 = 36$, and $(-6)^2 = 36$ ✓.

Which is the complete factoring of $6x^2 + 12x - 18$?

  • $6(x^2 + 2x - 3)$
  • $(6x - 6)(x + 3)$
  • $6(x - 3)(x + 1)$
  • $6(x + 3)(x - 1)$

Answer: $6(x + 3)(x - 1)$

Taking out $6$ gives $6(x^2 + 2x - 3)$, and the bracket has product $-3$ and sum $2$, so it is $(x + 3)(x - 1)$. The first option stops one step early, with a trinomial that still factors. The second is equal to the original, but its first bracket $6x - 6$ still has the factor $6$, so it is not complete. The third has the right numbers with the wrong signs, which expands to $6x^2 - 12x - 18$. Check the chosen one at $x = 1$: the original gives $6 + 12 - 18 = 0$, and the factors give $6 \cdot 4 \cdot 0 = 0$ ✓, while the third option gives $6(-2)(2) = -24$.

For which values of $x$ is $\dfrac{x + 4}{x^2 - x - 12}$ undefined? Give the values, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = -3$ and $x = 4$

The bottom factors as $(x - 4)(x + 3)$, which is zero at $x = 4$ and $x = -3$. Check by putting each value into the bottom: $16 - 4 - 12 = 0$ and $9 + 3 - 12 = 0$ ✓. The top plays no part in this, since the excluded values come from the bottom alone.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 + x - 12}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{x + 2}{x + 4}$

The top is $(x - 3)(x + 2)$ and the bottom is $(x + 4)(x - 3)$, so dividing out $x - 3$ leaves $\frac{x + 2}{x + 4}$, for $x \ne 3$ and $x \ne -4$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{1 - 1 - 6}{1 + 1 - 12} = \frac{-6}{-10} = \frac{3}{5}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{3}{5}$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 36}{x^2 + 12x + 36}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{x - 6}{x + 6}$

The top is $(x - 6)(x + 6)$ and the bottom is $(x + 6)^2 = (x + 6)(x + 6)$, so dividing out one $x + 6$ leaves $\frac{x - 6}{x + 6}$, for $x \ne -6$. Check at $x = 2$: the original gives $\frac{4 - 36}{4 + 24 + 36} = \frac{-32}{64} = -\frac{1}{2}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-4}{8} = -\frac{1}{2}$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{2 - x}{x^2 - 3x + 2}$.

Answer: $-\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$

The bottom is $(x - 1)(x - 2)$, and the top is $2 - x = -(x - 2)$, so dividing out $x - 2$ leaves $\frac{-1}{x - 1}$, for $x \ne 1$ and $x \ne 2$. Check at $x = 3$: the original gives $\frac{2 - 3}{9 - 9 + 2} = -\frac{1}{2}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-1}{2}$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{10x^2 - 15x}{5x^2}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{2x - 3}{x}$

The top is $5x(2x - 3)$ and the bottom is $5x \cdot x$, so dividing out $5x$ leaves $\frac{2x - 3}{x}$, for $x \ne 0$. Check at $x = 2$: the original gives $\frac{40 - 30}{20} = \frac{1}{2}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{4 - 3}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + 3x - 10}{x^2 - 4}$, and then evaluate the result at $x = 4$. Give the value as a decimal.

Answer: $1.5$

The top is $(x + 5)(x - 2)$ and the bottom is $(x - 2)(x + 2)$, so the fraction simplifies to $\frac{x + 5}{x + 2}$. At $x = 4$ that is $\frac{9}{6} = 1.5$. Check by evaluating the original directly: $\frac{16 + 12 - 10}{16 - 4} = \frac{18}{12} = 1.5$ ✓.

A student works on $\dfrac{x^2 - 5x + 4}{x^2 - 16}$. Line 1: $\dfrac{x^2 - 5x + 4}{x^2 - 16} = \dfrac{(x - 1)(x - 4)}{(x - 4)(x + 4)}$. Line 2: $= \dfrac{x - 1}{x + 4}$. Line 3: “The original is undefined only at $x = -4$.” Which statement about the work is correct?

  • Line 1 is wrong, because the top factors as $(x + 1)(x + 4)$.
  • Line 1 is wrong, because the bottom factors as $(x - 4)^2$.
  • Line 2 is wrong, because a factor cannot be divided out of both the top and the bottom.
  • Line 3 is wrong, because the original is also undefined at $x = 4$.

Answer: Line 3 is wrong, because the original is also undefined at $x = 4$.

Line 1 is right: $(x - 1)(x - 4) = x^2 - 5x + 4$ and $(x - 4)(x + 4) = x^2 - 16$, while $(x + 1)(x + 4)$ expands to $x^2 + 5x + 4$, the wrong middle sign, and $(x - 4)^2 = x^2 - 8x + 16$ is a perfect square, not a difference of squares. Line 2 is right too, since the shared factor $x - 4$ divides out. Line 3 forgets that the excluded values are read from the factored bottom before canceling: the bottom is zero at $x = 4$ as well as $x = -4$. Check by putting $x = 4$ into the original: the bottom is $16 - 16 = 0$, so the original is undefined there, though the simplified form $\frac{x - 1}{x + 4}$ is defined and equals $\frac{3}{8}$.

Recap

Back to the puzzle

The fraction equals $x + 1$ at every value of $x$ except $x = 1$. Factoring the top gives $\dfrac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1}$, and for every $x \ne 1$ the factor $x - 1$ is not zero and divides out, leaving $x + 1$. At $x = 1$ the bottom is $0$, so the fraction is undefined, while $x + 1$ is $2$. A second route: at $x = 0$ the fraction is $\frac{-1}{-1} = 1$ and $x + 1 = 1$ ✓, and at $x = 1$ the fraction is $\frac{0}{0}$, which is not a number. The tempting answer “yes, for every value” trusts the values that agree and ignores the one that does not; simplifying does not make an excluded value allowed.

  • Factoring is multiplication run backward. Since $(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq$, the trinomial $x^2 + bx + c$ factors when two numbers have product $c$ and sum $b$; with $c > 0$ both take the sign of $b$, and with $c < 0$ the signs are opposite and the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$.
  • Take a common factor out first, and factor completely: no factor may factor again.
  • For $ax^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term into them and group; both groups must leave the same bracket.
  • $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ because the middle terms cancel, and $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a + b)^2$; a sum of squares such as $x^2 + 9$ does not factor over the real numbers.
  • Check a factoring by expanding back. Wrong signs still look right until the middle term is expanded.
  • A rational expression is a fraction of polynomials. Values that make the bottom zero are excluded, found from the factored bottom before canceling, and they stay excluded after.
  • Simplify by factoring top and bottom completely and dividing out common factors. Only factors cancel; $\dfrac{x + 3}{x}$ is not $3$.
  • $a - b = -(b - a)$, so opposite factors cancel to $-1$, as in $\dfrac{3 - x}{x - 3} = -1$ for $x \ne 3$.
  • Check a simplified form by evaluating both forms at an allowed value; they must agree.
