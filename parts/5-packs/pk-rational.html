Factoring and Rational Expressions

Multiplying two brackets is a routine you know, and factoring is the same routine run backward: given the product, find the brackets. This pack starts there. It reads a trinomial with a leading coefficient of one as two numbers with a product and a sum, takes a common factor out first, splits the middle term of a trinomial with a larger leading coefficient, and meets the two special forms, the difference of squares and the perfect square, each argued from expanding and each checked by expanding back. Its second section puts factoring to work on fractions whose top and bottom are polynomials. Such a fraction is undefined where its bottom is zero, it simplifies by dividing out common factors and never common terms, and a factor that is the opposite of another gives minus one. The third section multiplies, divides, adds and subtracts such fractions, factoring first and dividing out before multiplying, multiplying by the reciprocal to divide, and using a least common denominator to add. The fourth uses that denominator to clear the fractions of an equation, and checks every candidate, since a solution that is an excluded value is extraneous.

A puzzle to carry through the pack

Try $x = 3$ in $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ and you get $\frac{8}{2} = 4$, which is the same as $x + 1 = 4$. Is the fraction $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ equal to $x + 1$?

  • Yes, for every value of $x$
  • Yes, except at one value of $x$
  • No, they only agree at some values

One test value agreeing does not settle it, and the question hides a trap in the single value of $x$ that makes the bottom of the fraction zero. The first section learns to write $x^2 - 1$ as a product of brackets, and the second uses that product to cancel, and to say exactly where the canceled fraction and the original stop agreeing. The last two sections add, multiply, divide and solve with such fractions, and the excluded value follows every step.

By the end of this pack you can factor a trinomial $x^2 + bx + c$ by finding two numbers with product $c$ and sum $b$, take a common factor out first, factor $ax^2 + bx + c$ by splitting the middle term, factor a difference of squares and a perfect square, and check any factoring by expanding back. You can name the values of $x$ that a rational expression excludes, simplify it by factoring top and bottom completely and dividing out common factors, handle an opposite factor, and check a simplified form by evaluating both forms at an allowed value. You can multiply and divide rational expressions by factoring first, add and subtract them over a least common denominator, and solve a rational equation by clearing the fractions, rejecting any extraneous solution.
Before you start

This pack leans on solving factored equations (Chapter 4, where a product equal to zero has a zero factor) and on reducing fractions by a common factor (Chapter 1). Its polynomials are the ones of Chapter 12. You can read it at any point alongside the course.

Three quick ones to wake up what the pack stands on. They are not scored. If one doesn't come to you, its solution says where to look.

Evaluate $(x + 3)(x - 2)$ when $x = 5$.

Answer: $24$

$x + 3 = 8$ and $x - 2 = 3$, so the product is $8 \cdot 3 = 24$. Check by expanding first: $x^2 + x - 6$ at $x = 5$ is $25 + 5 - 6 = 24$ ✓.

Write $\dfrac{18}{30}$ in lowest terms.

Answer: $\frac{3}{5}$

Both $18$ and $30$ are multiples of $6$: $\frac{18}{30} = \frac{3 \cdot 6}{5 \cdot 6} = \frac{3}{5}$, because $\frac{6}{6} = 1$. Check as decimals: $18 \div 30 = 0.6$ and $3 \div 5 = 0.6$ ✓.

Solve $(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0$. Give the two values of $x$, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = -1$ and $x = 4$

The product is zero when a factor is zero: $x - 4 = 0$ gives $x = 4$ and $x + 1 = 0$ gives $x = -1$. Check: at $x = 4$ the first bracket is $0$, and at $x = -1$ the second is $0$, so the product is $0$ both times ✓.

H.1 Factoring trinomials and special forms

To factor a polynomial is to write it as a product of simpler polynomials, and the way to learn it is to look at the multiplication it undoes. Expand $(x + p)(x + q)$ by giving each term of the first bracket to the second: $$(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + qx + px + pq = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq.$$ Read from right to left, that is the whole method for $x^2 + bx + c$: it factors as $(x + p)(x + q)$ when two numbers $p$ and $q$ have product $c$ and sum $b$. List the pairs of whole numbers whose product is $c$ and pick the pair whose sum is $b$. The signs tell you where to look. If $c > 0$, then $p$ and $q$ have the same sign, and so does their sum, so both take the sign of $b$: for $x^2 - 7x + 10$ both are negative, and $-2$ and $-5$ have product $10$ and sum $-7$, giving $(x - 2)(x - 5)$. If $c < 0$, the signs are opposite, and the number with the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$: for $x^2 + 2x - 15$ the pairs with product $15$ are $1, 15$ and $3, 5$, and only $3$ and $5$ differ by $2$, so the larger one, $5$, is positive: $(x - 3)(x + 5)$.

A common factor comes out first, always. In $3x^2 + 12x$ both terms are multiples of $3x$, so $3x^2 + 12x = 3x(x + 4)$. To factor completely means to keep going until no factor can be factored again. Taking the common factor out first also shrinks the numbers you then have to search.

When the leading coefficient is more than $1$, expand $(ax + p)(x + q)$ the same way: $ax^2 + aqx + px + pq$. The middle term is two pieces, $aqx$ and $px$. Their coefficients add to $b$, and multiply to $aq \cdot p = a \cdot pq = ac$. So for $ax^2 + bx + c$ look for two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term into those two pieces, and group. For $3x^2 + 10x + 8$ the product is $ac = 24$ and the sum is $10$, which are $4$ and $6$: $3x^2 + 4x + 6x + 8 = x(3x + 4) + 2(3x + 4) = (3x + 4)(x + 2)$. The grouping works because both groups leave the same bracket; if they leave different ones, the pair was wrong.

Two forms are worth knowing on sight, and both come from expanding. In $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 + ab - ab - b^2$ the middle terms cancel, so $$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b),$$ a difference of squares: $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$. And $(a + b)^2 = (a + b)(a + b) = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, so a perfect square trinomial has its first and last terms as squares and its middle term as twice their product: $x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)^2$, since $25 = 5^2$ and $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. A sum of squares is different. $x^2 + 9$ would need two numbers with product $9$ and sum $0$; two numbers with sum $0$ are $p$ and $-p$, whose product $-p^2$ is never positive, so it can never be $9$. So $x^2 + 9$ does not factor over the real numbers.

Always check by expanding back. A factoring with the wrong signs still looks right: $(x + 2)(x - 3)$ and $(x - 2)(x + 3)$ use the same numbers, but the first expands to $x^2 - x - 6$ and the second to $x^2 + x - 6$, and only the middle term tells them apart.

Example 1. A trinomial with a negative last term

Factor $x^2 - 3x - 28$.

  1. There is no common factor. We need two numbers with product $-28$ and sum $-3$.
  2. The product is negative, so the signs are opposite, and the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$, which is negative. The pairs with product $28$ are $1, 28$ and $2, 14$ and $4, 7$, and only $4$ and $7$ differ by $3$.
  3. So the numbers are $-7$ and $4$, and $x^2 - 3x - 28 = (x - 7)(x + 4)$.
  4. Check by expanding: $(x - 7)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - 7x - 28 = x^2 - 3x - 28$ ✓. A second check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $1 - 3 - 28 = -30$, and the factors give $(-6)(5) = -30$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Had the signs been swapped, $(x + 7)(x - 4)$, the middle term would be $+3x$. The sign of $b$ decides which number is negative.

Factor $x^2 + 9x + 20$.

Answer: $(x + 4)(x + 5)$

The pairs with product $20$ are $1, 20$ and $2, 10$ and $4, 5$, and only $4 + 5 = 9$. Check by expanding: $(x + 4)(x + 5) = x^2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x^2 + 9x + 20$ ✓.

Factor $5x^2 + 17x + 6$.

Answer: $(5x + 2)(x + 3)$

Here $ac = 30$ and $b = 17$, and the numbers are $2$ and $15$. Then $5x^2 + 15x + 2x + 6 = 5x(x + 3) + 2(x + 3) = (5x + 2)(x + 3)$. Check by expanding: $5x^2 + 15x + 2x + 6 = 5x^2 + 17x + 6$ ✓.

Example 2. A common factor, then a difference of squares

Factor $12x^2 - 75$ completely.

  1. Both terms are multiples of $3$, so take it out first: $12x^2 - 75 = 3(4x^2 - 25)$.
  2. Inside the bracket, $4x^2 = (2x)^2$ and $25 = 5^2$, so it is a difference of squares with $a = 2x$ and $b = 5$: $4x^2 - 25 = (2x - 5)(2x + 5)$.
  3. So $12x^2 - 75 = 3(2x - 5)(2x + 5)$. No factor factors again ($2x - 5$ and $2x + 5$ have no common factor), so this is complete.
  4. Check by expanding: $(2x - 5)(2x + 5) = 4x^2 - 25$, and $3(4x^2 - 25) = 12x^2 - 75$ ✓. A second check at $x = 1$: $12 - 75 = -63$, and $3(-3)(7) = -63$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Stopping at $3(4x^2 - 25)$ is correct as far as it goes but not complete, since the bracket still factors.

Factor $x^2 - 64$.

Answer: $(x - 8)(x + 8)$

With $a = x$ and $b = 8$, $x^2 - 64 = (x - 8)(x + 8)$. Check by expanding: $x^2 + 8x - 8x - 64 = x^2 - 64$ ✓.

H.2 Simplifying rational expressions

A rational expression is a fraction whose top and bottom are polynomials, and it behaves like a numeric fraction. The number fraction $\frac{6}{8}$ equals $\frac{3}{4}$ because $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3 \cdot 2}{4 \cdot 2} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{2}$ and $\frac{2}{2} = 1$: a common factor divides out. The same works for polynomials, once they are factored.

One thing comes before canceling. Division by zero is undefined, so any value of $x$ that makes the bottom zero is excluded. Find the excluded values from the factored bottom, before canceling, and they stay excluded after: simplifying never makes an excluded value allowed. This is what the puzzle turns on. Factor the top and the bottom completely: $$\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1}.$$ The bottom is zero at $x = 1$, so $x = 1$ is excluded. For every other $x$ the factor $x - 1$ is not zero, and $\frac{x - 1}{x - 1} = 1$, so the fraction equals $x + 1$. The two are equal at every allowed $x$, but not at $x = 1$, where the fraction is undefined.

To simplify, then: factor top and bottom completely, name the excluded values, and divide out the common factors. Only factors cancel, never terms. It is tempting to cancel the $x$ in $\dfrac{x + 3}{x}$ and write $3$, but at $x = 1$ the fraction is $\frac{4}{1} = 4$ and not $3$. In $x + 3$ the $x$ is one term of a sum, not a factor of the whole top; the top has no common factor with $x$, so nothing cancels and the fraction is already simplified.

A subtraction can hide a common factor: $a - b = -(b - a)$, since reversing a subtraction flips the sign. So two factors that are opposites cancel to $-1$: $\dfrac{3 - x}{x - 3} = \dfrac{-(x - 3)}{x - 3} = -1$ for $x \ne 3$.

The check is to evaluate. The simplified form must give the same value as the original at every allowed $x$, so evaluate both at a number that is not excluded and compare. A different value means a slip. Equal values are good evidence, but the factoring check from H.1, expanding back, is what shows the factoring itself is right.

Example 3. Two trinomials that share a factor

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 + x - 6}$ and name the excluded values.

  1. Factor the top: product $6$, sum $5$, so $x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$.
  2. Factor the bottom: product $-6$, sum $1$, so the numbers are $3$ and $-2$ and $x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$.
  3. The bottom is zero when $x = -3$ or $x = 2$, so those two values are excluded.
  4. The common factor is $x + 3$. Dividing it out leaves $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 2}$, for $x \ne -3$ and $x \ne 2$.
  5. Check at $x = 4$, which is allowed: the original gives $\frac{16 + 20 + 6}{16 + 4 - 6} = \frac{42}{14} = 3$ and the simplified form gives $\frac{6}{2} = 3$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

The value $x = -3$ is excluded even though $x + 3$ is gone from the simplified form. The original is still undefined there.

For which values of $x$ is $\dfrac{x + 1}{x^2 - 3x - 10}$ undefined? Give the values, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = -2$ and $x = 5$

The bottom factors as $(x - 5)(x + 2)$, which is zero at $x = 5$ and $x = -2$. Check by putting each value into the bottom: $25 - 15 - 10 = 0$ and $4 + 6 - 10 = 0$ ✓.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 7x + 12}{x^2 - 9}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{x - 4}{x + 3}$

The top is $(x - 3)(x - 4)$ and the bottom is $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, so dividing out $x - 3$ leaves $\frac{x - 4}{x + 3}$, for $x \ne 3$ and $x \ne -3$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{1 - 7 + 12}{1 - 9} = \frac{6}{-8} = -\frac{3}{4}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-3}{4} = -\frac{3}{4}$ ✓.

Example 4. A common factor and an opposite factor

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 25}{10 - 2x}$ and name the excluded value.

  1. The top is a difference of squares: $x^2 - 25 = (x - 5)(x + 5)$.
  2. The bottom has the common factor $2$: $10 - 2x = 2(5 - x)$. The factor $5 - x$ is the opposite of $x - 5$, so $5 - x = -(x - 5)$ and the bottom is $-2(x - 5)$.
  3. The bottom is zero at $x = 5$, so $x = 5$ is excluded.
  4. Divide out $x - 5$: $\dfrac{(x - 5)(x + 5)}{-2(x - 5)} = \dfrac{x + 5}{-2} = -\dfrac{x + 5}{2}$, for $x \ne 5$.
  5. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{1 - 25}{10 - 2} = \frac{-24}{8} = -3$, and the simplified form gives $-\frac{6}{2} = -3$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Writing the bottom with the factor $x - 5$ showing, rather than $5 - x$, is what lets the two factors be seen as the same.

Simplify $\dfrac{7 - x}{x^2 - 49}$.

Answer: $-\dfrac{1}{x + 7}$

The bottom is $(x - 7)(x + 7)$ and the top is $7 - x = -(x - 7)$, so dividing out $x - 7$ leaves $\frac{-1}{x + 7}$, for $x \ne 7$ and $x \ne -7$. Check at $x = 1$: the original gives $\frac{6}{-48} = -\frac{1}{8}$, and the simplified form gives $\frac{-1}{8}$ ✓.

H.3 Multiplying, dividing, adding and subtracting

Rational expressions follow the rules of numeric fractions, so each rule is argued first with numbers. To multiply, multiply tops and multiply bottoms: $$\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd},$$ because a piece of size $\frac{1}{b}$ cut into $d$ equal pieces gives pieces of size $\frac{1}{bd}$, and $ac$ of them are taken. The better habit is to factor first and divide out before multiplying, since the factors of a product are the factors of the pieces. Multiplied straight, $\frac{6}{35} \cdot \frac{14}{9} = \frac{84}{315}$, and the common factor $21$ must then be found. Factored first, $\frac{2 \cdot 3}{5 \cdot 7} \cdot \frac{2 \cdot 7}{3 \cdot 3} = \frac{2 \cdot 2}{5 \cdot 3} = \frac{4}{15}$, and each canceled pair is seen at once. For polynomials, factor every top and bottom completely, divide out any factor that stands on a top and on a bottom, in either fraction, and multiply what is left. The excluded values come from every bottom that appears, including a bottom whose factor later cancels.

To divide, remember what division asks: $a \div b$ is the number that, times $b$, gives $a$. So $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{5}$ is the number $q$ with $q \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{4}$. Multiply both sides by $\frac{5}{2}$, and since $\frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5} = 1$, $q = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{15}{8}$. Dividing by a fraction multiplies by its reciprocal, and for expressions $\frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} = \frac{A}{B} \cdot \frac{D}{C}$. The reciprocal has $C$ as a bottom, and a bottom cannot be zero, so the top of the divisor adds excluded values that neither original bottom shows.

To add, the pieces must be the same size, which means a common denominator. Any common multiple of the bottoms works, but the least one keeps the numbers small. For $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4}$ the product of the bottoms, $24$, gives $\frac{4}{24} + \frac{6}{24} = \frac{10}{24}$, which still reduces, while $12$ gives $\frac{2}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$ at once. The least common denominator (LCD) is the product of every distinct factor at its highest power: $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ and $4 = 2^2$, so the LCD is $2^2 \cdot 3 = 12$. For expressions, factor each bottom and do the same. Build each fraction up by multiplying its top and bottom by the factors it is missing, which multiplies by $1$; then add the tops and keep the bottom, because the bottom only names the size of the piece: two twelfths plus three twelfths is five twelfths. The bottoms do not add. Adding them gives $\frac{1 + 1}{6 + 4} = \frac{1}{5}$, which is less than $\frac{1}{4}$ alone, so it cannot be the sum. Then simplify if the top and bottom share a factor. For example, $\frac{2}{x + 1} + \frac{3}{x}$ has the LCD $x(x + 1)$, so it is $\frac{2x}{x(x + 1)} + \frac{3(x + 1)}{x(x + 1)} = \frac{5x + 3}{x(x + 1)}$, and at $x = 2$ the pieces give $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{13}{6}$ and the result gives $\frac{13}{6}$.

To subtract, subtract the whole top. A minus sign in front of a fraction applies to every term of its top, so in $\dfrac{4x}{x + 3} - \dfrac{x - 5}{x + 3}$ the work is $\dfrac{4x - (x - 5)}{x + 3} = \dfrac{4x - x + 5}{x + 3} = \dfrac{3x + 5}{x + 3}$. The slip is to write $4x - x - 5$, which changes only the first sign of the bracket. Evaluating catches it: at $x = 2$ the pieces give $\frac{8}{5} - \frac{-3}{5} = \frac{11}{5}$, the correct result gives $\frac{11}{5}$, and the slip gives $\frac{1}{5}$. That is the check for every operation here: evaluate the pieces and the result at an allowed value, and the two must agree.

Example 5. A product that cancels

Multiply and simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 3x - 10} \cdot \dfrac{x + 5}{x + 3}$, and name the excluded values.

  1. Factor the top of the first fraction, a difference of squares: $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$. Factor its bottom with numbers of product $-10$ and sum $3$, which are $5$ and $-2$: $x^2 + 3x - 10 = (x + 5)(x - 2)$.
  2. The bottoms are zero at $x = -5$, $x = 2$ and $x = -3$, so those three values are excluded.
  3. Write the product as one fraction: $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 3)(x + 5)}{(x + 5)(x - 2)(x + 3)}$. The factors $x + 3$ and $x + 5$ stand on the top and on the bottom, so they divide out and $\dfrac{x - 3}{x - 2}$ is left.
  4. Check at $x = 4$, which is allowed: the first fraction is $\frac{16 - 9}{16 + 12 - 10} = \frac{7}{18}$, the second is $\frac{9}{7}$, and their product is $\frac{63}{126} = \frac{1}{2}$. The result gives $\frac{4 - 3}{4 - 2} = \frac{1}{2}$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

The value $x = -3$ is excluded although only $x - 3$ and $x - 2$ remain: the second fraction was undefined there before anything canceled.

Multiply and simplify $\dfrac{6}{x - 7} \cdot \dfrac{x - 7}{x + 8}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{6}{x + 8}$

The factor $x - 7$ is on a bottom and on a top, so it divides out and $\frac{6}{x + 8}$ is left, for $x \ne 7$ and $x \ne -8$. Check at $x = 1$: the pieces give $\frac{6}{-6} \cdot \frac{-6}{9} = (-1)\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{3}$, and the result gives $\frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$ ✓.

Divide and simplify $\dfrac{x}{x + 6} \div \dfrac{x}{x - 1}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 6}$

The quotient is $\frac{x}{x + 6} \cdot \frac{x - 1}{x}$, and the $x$ divides out, leaving $\frac{x - 1}{x + 6}$. The excluded values are $x = -6$, $x = 1$ and $x = 0$, the last because it makes the divisor's top zero. Check at $x = 2$: the pieces give $\frac{2}{8} \div \frac{2}{1} = \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{8}$, and the result gives $\frac{1}{8}$ ✓.

Example 6. A difference with unlike binomial bottoms

Write $\dfrac{3}{x - 4} - \dfrac{2}{x + 1}$ as a single fraction and simplify.

  1. The bottoms $x - 4$ and $x + 1$ share no factor, so the LCD is their product $(x - 4)(x + 1)$, and $x = 4$ and $x = -1$ are excluded.
  2. Build each fraction up by the factor it is missing: $\dfrac{3(x + 1)}{(x - 4)(x + 1)} - \dfrac{2(x - 4)}{(x - 4)(x + 1)}$.
  3. Subtract the whole second top, which changes both of its signs: $3(x + 1) - 2(x - 4) = 3x + 3 - 2x + 8 = x + 11$.
  4. The result is $\dfrac{x + 11}{(x - 4)(x + 1)}$. The top is zero only at $x = -11$, which is not a zero of the bottom, so nothing cancels and this is simplified.
  5. Check at $x = 2$: the pieces give $\frac{3}{-2} - \frac{2}{3} = -\frac{9}{6} - \frac{4}{6} = -\frac{13}{6}$, and the result gives $\frac{13}{(-2)(3)} = -\frac{13}{6}$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Had the bracket been dropped, $3x + 3 - 2x - 8$ would give $x - 5$, and the check would give $\frac{-3}{-6} = \frac{1}{2}$, not $-\frac{13}{6}$.

Write $\dfrac{5}{x} - \dfrac{3}{x + 2}$ as a single fraction over $x(x + 2)$. What is its numerator?

Answer: $2x + 10$

The numerator is $5(x + 2) - 3x = 5x + 10 - 3x = 2x + 10$. Check at $x = 1$: the pieces give $5 - 1 = 4$, and the single fraction gives $\frac{2 + 10}{1 \cdot 3} = 4$ ✓.

H.4 Rational equations

An equation can be cleared of fractions by multiplying both sides by the same nonzero number, which keeps the two sides equal. With numbers, $\frac{x}{3} + \frac{x}{4} = 7$ is cleared by $12$, the LCD: $4x + 3x = 84$, so $x = 12$, and $\frac{12}{3} + \frac{12}{4} = 4 + 3 = 7$ ✓. A rational equation has the unknown in a bottom, so the multiplier has $x$ in it, and that is where the care goes. Multiplying both sides by zero would make any equation true. The LCD is zero only at the excluded values, so for every allowed $x$ the multiplication is safe. The method is then: factor the bottoms, name the excluded values, multiply every term by the LCD so each term loses its bottom, solve the polynomial equation that is left, which is linear or a quadratic that factors (H.1 built that), and check each answer in the original equation. For $\frac{2}{x} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$ the LCD is $6x$ and $x \ne 0$, so $12 + 2x = 3x$ and $x = 12$; the check is $\frac{2}{12} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$ ✓.

The cleared equation is true wherever the original is, but it can be true in more places. If one of its solutions is an excluded value, the original has a zero bottom there and says nothing about numbers, so that candidate is extraneous and is not a solution. This is why every candidate is substituted into the original equation, not the cleared one. The check also catches arithmetic slips.

A proportion $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ is the simplest rational equation, with one fraction on each side. The LCD is $bd$, and multiplying both sides by it leaves $ad = cb$. So cross-multiplying is the LCD step in disguise, with $b \ne 0$ and $d \ne 0$ excluded, and nothing new.

Rate stories lead here. If one person finishes a job alone in $a$ hours and another in $b$ hours, together they do $\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}$ of the job each hour, and in $t$ hours together they do the whole job, so $$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t}.$$ This is the set-up of F.3. When one of the times is the unknown it stands in a bottom, and clearing the fractions with the LCD solves it. The answer must be positive and, with both working, less than each time alone.

Example 7. A candidate that fails the check

Solve $\dfrac{x^2}{x - 3} = \dfrac{9}{x - 3} + 2$.

  1. The only bottom is $x - 3$, so $x = 3$ is excluded.
  2. Multiply every term by $x - 3$: $x^2 = 9 + 2(x - 3)$, which is $x^2 = 2x + 3$.
  3. Move everything to one side: $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$. Numbers with product $-3$ and sum $-2$ are $-3$ and $1$, so $(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0$ and the candidates are $x = 3$ and $x = -1$.
  4. The candidate $x = 3$ is the excluded value: it makes the bottom $x - 3$ zero, so it is extraneous and is dropped.
  5. Check $x = -1$ in the original: the left side is $\frac{1}{-4} = -\frac{1}{4}$ and the right side is $\frac{9}{-4} + 2 = -\frac{9}{4} + \frac{8}{4} = -\frac{1}{4}$ ✓. The solution is $x = -1$. $\blacksquare$

The cleared equation $x^2 = 2x + 3$ does have $3$ as a solution ($9 = 9$). It is the original that has no value at $3$.

Solve $\dfrac{10}{x} = \dfrac{4}{x - 3}$.

Answer: $x = 5$

With $x \ne 0$ and $x \ne 3$, multiply both sides by $x(x - 3)$: $10(x - 3) = 4x$, so $10x - 30 = 4x$, $6x = 30$ and $x = 5$. Check: the left side is $\frac{10}{5} = 2$ and the right side is $\frac{4}{2} = 2$ ✓.

Solve $\dfrac{x^2}{x - 2} = \dfrac{4}{x - 2} + 5$. Give the one solution.

Answer: $x = 3$

Multiply by $x - 2$: $x^2 = 4 + 5(x - 2)$, so $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ and $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$. The candidate $x = 2$ makes the bottom zero, so it is extraneous. Check $x = 3$: the left side is $\frac{9}{1} = 9$ and the right side is $\frac{4}{1} + 5 = 9$ ✓.

Example 8. A work problem

Quinn can mow a lawn alone in $6$ hours. Working with Ravi, the lawn takes $4$ hours. How many hours would Ravi take alone?

  1. Let $x$ be Ravi's hours alone. Quinn does $\frac{1}{6}$ of the lawn each hour, Ravi does $\frac{1}{x}$, and together they do $\frac{1}{4}$: $\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, with $x \ne 0$.
  2. The LCD is $12x$. Multiply every term by it: $2x + 12 = 3x$.
  3. Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $x = 12$, which is allowed.
  4. Check in the story: Ravi does $\frac{1}{12}$ of the lawn per hour, and $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{2}{12} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$ ✓. It also makes sense that $12$ hours alone is longer than the $4$ hours together. Ravi takes $12$ hours. $\blacksquare$

The times $6$ and $12$ are not added or averaged. The rates, which are fractions of the same whole lawn, are.

Solve $x + \dfrac{6}{x} = 5$. Give the two values of $x$, separated by a comma.

Answer: $x = 2$ and $x = 3$

With $x \ne 0$, multiply every term by $x$: $x^2 + 6 = 5x$, so $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ and $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$. Check: $2 + \frac{6}{2} = 5$ and $3 + \frac{6}{3} = 5$ ✓, and neither value is $0$.

Factor $2x^2 + 14x + 24$ completely.

Answer: $2(x + 3)(x + 4)$

Every term is a multiple of $2$, so $2x^2 + 14x + 24 = 2(x^2 + 7x + 12)$. In the bracket, $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ and $3 + 4 = 7$, so it is $(x + 3)(x + 4)$. Check at $x = 1$: the trinomial gives $2 + 14 + 24 = 40$, and the factors give $2 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 40$ ✓.

What is the solution set of $\dfrac{x}{x - 5} = \dfrac{5}{x - 5} + 2$?

  • $x = 5$
  • $x = 7$
  • $x = -5$
  • There is no solution.

Answer: There is no solution.

With $x \ne 5$, multiply every term by $x - 5$: $x = 5 + 2(x - 5)$, so $x = 2x - 5$ and $x = 5$. But $x = 5$ is the excluded value, so it is extraneous, and no other candidate is left. Recap

Back to the puzzle

The fraction equals $x + 1$ at every value of $x$ except $x = 1$. Factoring the top gives $\dfrac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1}$, and for every $x \ne 1$ the factor $x - 1$ is not zero and divides out, leaving $x + 1$. At $x = 1$ the bottom is $0$, so the fraction is undefined, while $x + 1$ is $2$. A second route: at $x = 0$ the fraction is $\frac{-1}{-1} = 1$ and $x + 1 = 1$ ✓, and at $x = 1$ the fraction is $\frac{0}{0}$, which is not a number. The tempting answer “yes, for every value” trusts the values that agree and ignores the one that does not; simplifying does not make an excluded value allowed.

  • Factoring is multiplication run backward. Since $(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq$, the trinomial $x^2 + bx + c$ factors when two numbers have product $c$ and sum $b$; with $c > 0$ both take the sign of $b$, and with $c < 0$ the signs are opposite and the larger absolute value carries the sign of $b$.
  • Take a common factor out first, and factor completely: no factor may factor again.
  • For $ax^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term into them and group; both groups must leave the same bracket.
  • $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ because the middle terms cancel, and $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a + b)^2$; a sum of squares such as $x^2 + 9$ does not factor over the real numbers.
  • Check a factoring by expanding back. Wrong signs still look right until the middle term is expanded.
  • A rational expression is a fraction of polynomials. Values that make the bottom zero are excluded, found from the factored bottom before canceling, and they stay excluded after.
  • Simplify by factoring top and bottom completely and dividing out common factors. Only factors cancel; $\dfrac{x + 3}{x}$ is not $3$.
  • $a - b = -(b - a)$, so opposite factors cancel to $-1$, as in $\dfrac{3 - x}{x - 3} = -1$ for $x \ne 3$.
  • Check a simplified form by evaluating both forms at an allowed value; they must agree.
  • To multiply, factor first, divide out any factor on a top and a bottom, and multiply what is left; the excluded values come from every bottom that appears, before canceling.
  • To divide, multiply by the reciprocal, since $a \div b$ is the number that times $b$ gives $a$; the top of the divisor is excluded too.
  • To add or subtract, use the least common denominator, the product of every distinct factor at its highest power; build each fraction up, add the tops (or subtract the whole top, changing every sign) and keep the bottom. Bottoms never add.
  • To solve a rational equation, multiply every term by the LCD, solve what is left, and check each candidate in the original: a candidate equal to an excluded value is extraneous. Cross-multiplying a proportion is this step, and $\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t}$ is the work equation.
