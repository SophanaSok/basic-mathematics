Linear Models and Inequalities

Many quantities start somewhere and then change by the same amount at every step: a taxi fare, a draining tank, a plant, a temperature that falls through the evening. This pack turns that picture into algebra. It first says what the slope of a line means as a rate of change, with its units, and why any two points of a line give the same one. Then it writes the model $y = mx + b$ from a story, from two points or from a table, and uses it to predict a value and to find when a quantity reaches a target. Later sections take up inequalities in one and two unknowns, and systems of inequalities.

A puzzle to carry through the pack

A taxi ride of $3$ miles costs $\$11$, and a ride of $5$ miles costs $\$17$. If the fare follows a straight line, what does a ride of $10$ miles cost?

  • $\$22$
  • $\$32$
  • $\$34$

Doubling the fare for $5$ miles is tempting, and it fails, because part of the fare is charged before the taxi moves at all. Two rides are enough to find both the part that grows with the distance and the part that does not. The first section finds the part that grows, the rate of change. (The second section finds the fixed part and writes the whole fare as one expression. Inequalities come in the sections after that.)

By the end of this pack you can find the slope of a line from two points or from a table, even when the table's steps are uneven, say what a slope means in a sentence with its units, tell from its sign whether a quantity rises, falls or stays flat, read the rate and the starting value from $y = mx + b$, write the model from a story, from two points or from a table and check it at a point you did not use, predict a value with it, solve $mx + b = T$ to find when a quantity reaches a target, and say where a model stops making sense.

Compute $\dfrac{17 - 5}{6 - 2}$.

Answer: $3$

The top is $12$ and the bottom is $4$, and $12 \div 4 = 3$. Check: $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ ✓. This quotient of two differences is the shape of everything in the pack.

Evaluate $18 - 4x$ when $x = 3$.

Answer: $6$

$4 \cdot 3 = 12$, and $18 - 12 = 6$. Check: $6 + 12 = 18$ ✓.

Solve $3x + 4 = 19$.

Answer: $x = 5$

Subtract $4$ from both sides to get $3x = 15$, then divide both sides by $3$. See §2.1. Check: $3 \cdot 5 + 4 = 19$ ✓.

E.1 Slope as a rate of change

Take two points on a line, with $x$ across and $y$ up. Going from the first to the second, $x$ changes by some amount, the run, and $y$ changes by some amount, the rise, which is negative when $y$ falls. The slope of the line is the rise divided by the run: $$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}.$$ Subtract in the same order on the top and the bottom. Swap the two points and both differences change sign, so the quotient is the same.

Does it matter which two points you pick? It does not, and this is why slope belongs to the line and not to the pair. Draw the rise-and-run triangle for one pair of points, and another for a second pair on the same line. In each, the run is horizontal, the rise is vertical and the line itself is the slanted side. Both triangles have a right angle. The horizontal sides are parallel, so the line meets them at the same angle in each triangle. Two equal angles make the triangles similar, and similar triangles have their sides in the same ratio, so rise over run comes out the same in both. If the line falls, the triangle sits below the horizontal and the rise is negative, and the argument is the same.

Now use it. Take a run of exactly $1$: the rise is then $m$ itself. So the slope says how much $y$ changes for one more $x$, and when the axes carry units, so does the slope: the units of $y$ per unit of $x$. A fare that rises $\$6$ over $2$ miles has slope $\frac{6}{2} = 3$ dollars per mile. This is the rate of change. A table of a straight line works the same way. Pick any two rows. If the steps in $x$ are not equal, divide by the actual gap in $x$ and not by the number of rows between them.

The sign tells the story. A positive slope means $y$ rises as $x$ grows, a negative slope means it falls, and a slope of zero means it stays flat, because a rise of zero over any run is zero. In the model $y = mx + b$, the number $m$ is the slope, and $b$ is the value of $y$ when $x = 0$, the starting value. For $y = 15 + 25h$, the cost $y$ of $h$ hours, each hour adds $25$ dollars and the start is $15$ dollars. The rate and the total are different things: the total changes from hour to hour, and the rate is how fast it changes.

Read the story with care. “$\$40$ for the first hour and $\$25$ for each hour after” gives a rate of $25$ dollars per hour, not $40$. At $1$ hour the cost is $40$, and one hour earlier, at $0$ hours, it would be $40 - 25 = 15$. (Lines get a full chapter later, in Chapter 10, and Pack B's scatterplots used the slope of a line of fit. Neither is needed here.)

Example 1. Slope from two points, with its unit

A balloon is $300$ feet up after $2$ minutes and $780$ feet up after $8$ minutes. At what rate is it rising?

  1. The rise is the change in height: $780 - 300 = 480$ feet. The run is the change in time: $8 - 2 = 6$ minutes.
  2. $m = \dfrac{480\text{ ft}}{6\text{ min}} = 80$ feet per minute.
  3. The slope is positive, and the balloon is going up, as the numbers said.
  4. Check by the other order: $\dfrac{300 - 780}{2 - 8} = \dfrac{-480}{-6} = 80$ ✓. Check by using it: $6$ minutes at $80$ feet per minute is $480$ feet, and $300 + 480 = 780$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

The unit of the answer comes from the question: feet on the top, minutes on the bottom.

A line passes through $(1, 4)$ and $(5, 12)$. What is its slope?

Answer: $2$

$\dfrac{12 - 4}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$. Check: four steps of $2$ take $y$ from $4$ to $4 + 8 = 12$ ✓.

The cost in dollars of renting a ladder for $h$ hours is $C = 12 + 7h$. How many dollars per hour does the cost change?

Answer: $7$ dollars per hour

The number that multiplies $h$ is the rate, $7$. The $12$ is the start. Check: at $0$ hours the cost is $12$, at $1$ hour it is $19$, and $19 - 12 = 7$ ✓.

Example 2. A table with uneven steps

The water level in a pond is measured on four days. Is it changing at a steady rate, and if so what is the rate?

Water level in a pond on four days
DayLevel (centimeters)
0$80$
2$74$
5$65$
9$53$
  1. The days are $2$, $3$ and $4$ apart, so compare each step with its own gap. Days $0$ to $2$: $\frac{74 - 80}{2 - 0} = -3$. Days $2$ to $5$: $\frac{65 - 74}{5 - 2} = -3$. Days $5$ to $9$: $\frac{53 - 65}{9 - 5} = -3$.
  2. All three agree, so the level changes at a steady rate: $-3$ centimeters per day.
  3. The slope is negative, so the pond is falling.
  4. Check from the ends: $\frac{53 - 80}{9 - 0} = \frac{-27}{9} = -3$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Each change in level goes with its own gap in days; a change from one step over the gap of another gives a wrong rate.

A parking garage charges $\$9$ for the first hour and $\$4$ for each hour after. What would the line through these charges give for $0$ hours, in dollars?

Answer: $\$5$

The cost at $1$ hour is $9$, and one hour earlier it is $4$ less: $9 - 4 = 5$. Check: the line $5 + 4h$ gives $9$ at $1$ hour and $13$ at $2$ hours, and $13 = 9 + 4$ ✓.

E.2 Writing a linear model

Many situations have a starting value and a constant rate. A bike rental costs $\$6$ to start and $\$4$ for each hour. The cost after $x$ hours is the start plus $x$ copies of the rate, because each hour adds the same amount: $6 + 4x$. In general a quantity that starts at $b$ and changes by $m$ for every one more $x$ is $$y = mx + b,$$ where $b$ is the value when $x = 0$ and $m$ is the change per one more $x$. When the quantity falls, $m$ is negative, and the model subtracts.

To write a model from a story, name the count with a letter, find the start (the value before anything has happened) and find the rate (the amount that repeats). Then write start plus rate times count, and read the units: dollars plus dollars per hour times hours leaves dollars.

To write it from two data points, find $m$ first, as in the last section. Then put one point and $m$ into $y = mx + b$ and solve for $b$. Why do two points settle the line? Two points fix the slope, by rise over run. And the slope together with one point fixes $b$: from that point, each step of $1$ in $x$ moves $y$ by $m$, so going back to $x = 0$ lands on exactly one value. Nothing is left to choose. A table works the same way, since two of its rows are two points.

Check the model against a point you did not use. The two points that built it fit by construction, so they prove nothing. A third point that fits is evidence, and one that does not means a slip, or a situation that is not linear.

Then use it. To predict, put a value of $x$ into the model. To answer a “when” question, set the model equal to the target and solve $mx + b = T$: subtract $b$ from both sides, then divide by $m$. Last, ask when the model makes sense. A count of tickets is a whole number that is not negative, and a plant does not grow at the same rate forever. Inside the range of the data the model is a good description. Beyond it, as Pack B said of lines of fit, it is a guess.

Example 3. A model from a table, checked at a third row

A tutoring service lists the total paid after some sessions. Write the total in dollars after $n$ sessions, assuming a fixed fee plus a price per session.

Total paid for tutoring sessions
SessionsTotal (dollars)
2$95$
5$215$
8$335$
  1. Use the first two rows. The rate is $m = \frac{215 - 95}{5 - 2} = \frac{120}{3} = 40$ dollars per session.
  2. Put the first row into $y = 40n + b$: $95 = 40 \cdot 2 + b$, so $b = 95 - 80 = 15$.
  3. The model is $y = 40n + 15$: a fee of $\$15$ and $\$40$ for each session.
  4. Check at the third row, which was not used: $40 \cdot 8 + 15 = 335$ ✓. Check $b$ from the other row: $215 - 40 \cdot 5 = 15$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

The fee $b$ is the value at $0$ sessions, which the table never lists. The model finds it.

A sprout is $5$ centimeters tall and grows $3$ centimeters each week. Write its height in centimeters after $n$ weeks, in terms of $n$.

Answer: $3n + 5$

Start $5$, plus $3$ for each of the $n$ weeks. Check at $n = 2$: the formula gives $11$, and the story gives $5 + 3 + 3 = 11$ ✓.

A candle's height in centimeters after $t$ hours is $H = 20 - 2.5t$. How tall is it after $6$ hours?

Answer: $5$ centimeters

$20 - 2.5 \cdot 6 = 20 - 15 = 5$. Check hour by hour: $17.5$, $15$, $12.5$, $10$, $7.5$, $5$ ✓.

Example 4. When does it reach the target?

A tank holds $260$ gallons and drains $8$ gallons each minute. After how many minutes does it hold $100$ gallons? For how long does the model make sense?

  1. The model is $G = 260 - 8t$, with $t$ in minutes. We want $G = 100$.
  2. $260 - 8t = 100$. Subtract $260$ from both sides: $-8t = -160$. Divide by $-8$: $t = 20$ minutes.
  3. The model makes sense while the tank has water in it, from $t = 0$ until $G = 0$, that is until $t = 260 \div 8 = 32.5$ minutes.
  4. Check by substituting: $260 - 8 \cdot 20 = 100$ ✓. Check another way: the tank must lose $260 - 100 = 160$ gallons, and at $8$ gallons a minute that takes $160 \div 8 = 20$ minutes ✓. $\blacksquare$

After $32.5$ minutes the formula would give a negative amount of water, which is impossible. The domain of the model is the range where the story holds.

A line has slope $4$ and passes through $(3, 20)$. What is $b$, the value of $y$ when $x = 0$?

Answer: $8$

$20 = 4 \cdot 3 + b$, so $b = 20 - 12 = 8$. Check: $4 \cdot 3 + 8 = 20$ ✓.

Practice

Give each answer as a number in the unit the question names, without writing the unit. Use a decimal, such as $4.5$, where the answer is not whole, and a negative number where the quantity falls. Where a question asks for an expression, use the letter it names. Money is in dollars.

Find the slope of the line through $(2, 9)$ and $(5, 21)$.

Answer: $4$

$\dfrac{21 - 9}{5 - 2} = \dfrac{12}{3} = 4$. Check in the other order: $\dfrac{9 - 21}{2 - 5} = \dfrac{-12}{-3} = 4$ ✓.

Find the slope of the line through $(-3, 10)$ and $(1, -2)$.

Answer: $-3$

$\dfrac{-2 - 10}{1 - (-3)} = \dfrac{-12}{4} = -3$. Check in the other order: $\dfrac{10 - (-2)}{-3 - 1} = \dfrac{12}{-4} = -3$ ✓. The line falls as $x$ grows, so a negative slope fits.

Find the slope of the line through $(-2, 1)$ and $(3, 5)$. Give it as a fraction.

Answer: $\dfrac45$

$\dfrac{5 - 1}{3 - (-2)} = \dfrac{4}{5}$, already in lowest terms. Check in the other order: $\dfrac{1 - 5}{-2 - 3} = \dfrac{-4}{-5} = \dfrac45$ ✓.

A plumber's bill is $\$140$ for a $2$-hour job and $\$260$ for a $4$-hour job. If the bill follows a straight line, what is the plumber's rate of change, in dollars per hour?

Answer: $60$ dollars per hour

$\dfrac{260 - 140}{4 - 2} = \dfrac{120}{2} = 60$. Check: two more hours at $60$ is $120$, and $140 + 120 = 260$ ✓.

A gardener measures a plant. What is its rate of change, in centimeters per week?

Answer: $3.5$ centimeters per week

From week $1$ to week $7$: $\dfrac{35 - 14}{7 - 1} = \dfrac{21}{6} = 3.5$. Check with the other rows, each over its own gap: $\frac{17.5 - 14}{1} = 3.5$, $\frac{24.5 - 17.5}{2} = 3.5$ and $\frac{35 - 24.5}{3} = 3.5$ ✓.

A tank holds $400$ gallons after $4$ minutes of draining and $325$ gallons after $9$ minutes. What is the rate of change of the water, in gallons per minute?

Answer: $-15$ gallons per minute

$\dfrac{325 - 400}{9 - 4} = \dfrac{-75}{5} = -15$. Check: $5$ minutes at $-15$ is $-75$, and $400 - 75 = 325$ ✓.

The temperature in degrees Fahrenheit $h$ hours after midnight is $T = 62 - 1.5h$. By how many degrees does it change over $6$ hours? Use a negative number for a fall.

Answer: $-9$ degrees

The rate is $-1.5$ degrees per hour, and $-1.5 \cdot 6 = -9$. Check: $T(0) = 62$ and $T(6) = 62 - 9 = 53$, and $53 - 62 = -9$ ✓.

A gym charges a $\$35$ membership fee and $\$20$ each month. Write the total cost in dollars after $n$ months, in terms of $n$.

Answer: $20n + 35$

The fee is the start, $35$, and each of the $n$ months adds $20$. Check at $n = 2$: the formula gives $75$, and the story gives $35 + 20 + 20 = 75$ ✓.

A phone battery is at $100$ percent and loses $6$ percent each hour. Write the percent left after $t$ hours, in terms of $t$.

Answer: $100 - 6t$

Start at $100$ and take away $6$ for each of the $t$ hours. Check at $t = 3$: the formula gives $100 - 18 = 82$, and the story gives $100 - 6 - 6 - 6 = 82$ ✓.

A taxi company lists two fares. If the fare follows a straight line, what is the starting charge, the fare for $0$ miles, in dollars?

Taxi fares for two rides
Distance (miles)Fare (dollars)
2$10$

Answer: $\$3$

The rate is $\frac{24 - 10}{6 - 2} = \frac{14}{4} = 3.5$ dollars per mile, and $10 = 3.5 \cdot 2 + b$ gives $b = 10 - 7 = 3$. Check with the other row: $24 - 3.5 \cdot 6 = 24 - 21 = 3$ ✓.

A plant is $19$ centimeters tall after $3$ weeks and $31$ centimeters tall after $6$ weeks. Assuming it keeps growing at the same steady rate, how many centimeters tall is it after $10$ weeks?

Answer: $47$ centimeters

The rate is $\frac{31 - 19}{6 - 3} = 4$ centimeters per week, and $b = 19 - 4 \cdot 3 = 7$, so $H = 7 + 4n$, with $n$ the number of weeks, and $H(10) = 47$. Check from week $6$: four more weeks at $4$ is $16$, and $31 + 16 = 47$ ✓. This is beyond the data, so it is a prediction and not a measurement.

A savings account holds $\$150$ and gains $\$35$ each week. After how many weeks does it hold $\$500$?

Answer: $10$ weeks

$150 + 35w = 500$, so $35w = 350$ and $w = 10$. Check: $150 + 35 \cdot 10 = 500$ ✓.

At 4 p.m. the temperature is $76$°F and it falls $4$°F each hour. After how many hours does it reach $40$°F?

Answer: $9$ hours

$76 - 4h = 40$, so $4h = 36$ and $h = 9$. Check: $76 - 4 \cdot 9 = 40$ ✓.

A repair shop charges $\$55$ for the first hour and $\$35$ for each hour after. Write the charge in dollars for a job of $h$ hours, in terms of $h$.

Answer: $35h + 20$

The rate is $35$ dollars per hour. At $1$ hour the charge is $55$, so one hour earlier it would be $55 - 35 = 20$, the start. Check at $h = 3$: the formula gives $20 + 105 = 125$, and the story gives $55 + 35 + 35 = 125$ ✓.

Recap

Back to the puzzle

A ride of $10$ miles costs $\$32$. The rate is $\frac{17 - 11}{5 - 3} = 3$ dollars per mile, and from $11 = 3 \cdot 3 + b$ the fixed charge is $b = 2$ dollars, so the fare is $2 + 3x$ and $2 + 30 = 32$. A second route: from the $5$-mile ride, five more miles add $5 \cdot 3 = 15$, and $17 + 15 = 32$. Doubling $\$17$ gives $34$ for $10$ miles, which is too much, because it counts the fixed charge twice.

  • The slope is $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, with the points subtracted in the same order, and it is the same for any two points of a line because the rise-and-run triangles are similar.
  • The slope means how much $y$ changes for one more $x$, in units of $y$ per unit of $x$. A positive slope rises, a negative one falls, and zero is flat.
  • From a table, use the actual gap in $x$. From a story such as “$\$40$ for the first hour and $\$25$ for each hour after”, the rate is the amount that repeats.
  • In $y = mx + b$, $m$ is the rate and $b$ is the value at $x = 0$. The total changes, and the rate is how fast.
  • Write a model as start plus rate times count. Two points fix a line: the slope first, then $b$ from one point.
  • Check a model at a point you did not use, predict by putting in a value, and find when by solving $mx + b = T$.
  • A model makes sense for the counts that occur, and beyond the data it is a guess.
