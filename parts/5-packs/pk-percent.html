Ratios, Rates and Percent

Comparing two quantities is the most common thing a word problem asks of you, and the exams ask it constantly. This pack says what a ratio, a rate and a percent are, why the rules for working with them hold, and how to keep a problem from fooling you.

A puzzle to carry through the pack

Five machines make five widgets in five minutes. How long would it take one hundred machines to make one hundred widgets?

  • 100 minutes
  • 20 minutes
  • 5 minutes

Most people's first answer is wrong. The idea of a rate, in the first section, settles it in two lines. (And if a price rises 20% and then falls 20%, is it back where it began? The third section settles that one.)

By the end of this pack you can write a ratio in lowest terms and tell a part-to-part ratio from a part-to-whole one, find and compare unit rates, solve a proportion and say why cross-multiplying works, find a percent of a quantity, the whole from a part, or the percent itself, work out a percent change and undo it, combine repeated changes by multiplying their multipliers, and spot the number in a word problem that does not belong to the question.
Before you start

This pack leans on fractions as single numbers (Chapter 1) and on the two legal moves for an equation (Chapter 2). You need no other part of the course, and you can read the pack at any point alongside it.

Three quick ones to wake up what the pack stands on. They are not scored. If one doesn't come to you, its solution says where to look.

Write $\dfrac{18}{24}$ in lowest terms.

Answer: $\dfrac34$

The largest number that divides both $18$ and $24$ is $6$, and $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac34$. See §1.5. A ratio is simplified the same way.

Compute $12 \div \dfrac34$.

Answer: $16$

Dividing by $\frac34$ is multiplying by its inverse $\frac43$: $12 \cdot \frac43 = 16$. See §1.6. Rates are quotients, and this is the move behind them.

Solve $\dfrac{x}{4} = 3$.

Answer: $x = 12$

Multiply both sides by $4$, which is not zero: $x = 12$. See §2.1. Solving a proportion is this move done twice.

A.1 Ratios and rates

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by saying how many there are of one for the other. A bag holds $8$ red marbles and $12$ blue ones, so the ratio of red to blue is $8 : 12$. Shrink the bag to $2$ red and $3$ blue and nothing about the comparison has changed, so $8 : 12$ and $2 : 3$ are the same ratio. The reason is that a ratio records only how the two numbers compare, and a comparison survives multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same nonzero number. That is exactly the rule for equal fractions in Chapter 1, and it gives the rule for simplifying: divide both terms by their greatest common factor. When no number but $1$ divides both, the ratio is in lowest terms. Here the common factor is $4$, and $8 : 12$ becomes $2 : 3$.

Two kinds of ratio hide behind the same words. Red to blue, $2 : 3$, compares one part with another. Red to all the marbles compares a part with the whole, and the whole is $2 + 3 = 5$ marbles, so the ratio is $2 : 5$. As a fraction, red is $\frac25$ of the marbles, not $\frac23$. Most slips come from counting the wrong whole, so read the words after "to" and ask what they count. Order matters too: blue to red is $3 : 2$, a different ratio.

A rate is a ratio of two quantities of different kinds, such as miles and hours or dollars and ounces, written with the word "per". To find it, divide. If a car goes $150$ miles in $3$ hours, then $150 \div 3 = 50$ miles for each hour, or $50$ miles per hour. A rate with "one" underneath is a unit rate. It matters because two rates that count different amounts cannot be compared as they stand: is $12$ ounces for $\$3.00$ a better price than $20$ ounces for $\$4.60$? Dividing each by its amount turns two statements about different amounts into two statements about the same amount, one ounce, and now the prices can be set side by side.

Units are a check on which way to divide. Carry them through like numbers: miles divided by hours is miles per hour, and $50$ miles per hour times $2$ hours is $100$ miles, because the hours cancel. If the units you end with are not the ones the question asks for, you divided the wrong way round. And if the units of the time differ from the units of the rate, convert before dividing: $30$ minutes is half an hour, not $30$ hours.

Example 1. The better buy

A $12$-ounce box of cereal costs $\$3.00$, and a $20$-ounce box costs $\$4.60$. Which box is cheaper per ounce, and by how much?

  1. Work in cents, so the division stays in whole numbers: $\$3.00$ is $300$ cents and $\$4.60$ is $460$ cents.
  2. Small box: $300 \div 12 = 25$ cents per ounce.
  3. Large box: $460 \div 20 = 23$ cents per ounce.
  4. Check by multiplying back: $25 \cdot 12 = 300$ ✓ and $23 \cdot 20 = 460$ ✓. The large box is cheaper, by $2$ cents per ounce. $\blacksquare$

Neither total price nor box size answers the question alone. Only the price for one ounce does, and a bigger box is not always the cheaper one.

A team has won $12$ games and lost $8$. What is the ratio of games won to games played, in lowest terms?

Answer: $3 : 5$

Games played: $12 + 8 = 20$. The ratio is $12 : 20$, and dividing both terms by $4$ gives $3 : 5$. Won to lost would have been $12 : 8 = 3 : 2$, a different ratio.

Example 2. A rate in the wrong units

A runner covers $2.4$ miles in $18$ minutes. What is her speed in miles per hour?

  1. The answer is wanted per hour, so change the time first: $18$ minutes is $\frac{18}{60} = \frac{3}{10}$ of an hour.
  2. Divide the distance by the time: $2.4 \div \frac{3}{10} = 2.4 \cdot \frac{10}{3} = 8$.
  3. Check by running it forward: $8$ miles per hour for $\frac{3}{10}$ of an hour is $8 \cdot \frac{3}{10} = 2.4$ miles ✓. Her speed is $8$ miles per hour. $\blacksquare$

Dividing $2.4$ by $18$ would give miles per minute, about $0.133$, a perfectly good rate but not the one asked for. The units gave it away.

A hose fills a $45$-gallon tub in $9$ minutes. How many gallons does it deliver per minute?

Answer: $5$ gallons per minute

$45 \div 9 = 5$. Check: $5$ gallons per minute for $9$ minutes is $5 \cdot 9 = 45$ gallons ✓.

Cereal A is $15$ ounces for $\$3.30$. Cereal B is $24$ ounces for $\$5.04$. What is the lower price per ounce, in cents?

Answer: $21$ cents per ounce

A: $330 \div 15 = 22$ cents. B: $504 \div 24 = 21$ cents. B is cheaper per ounce, at $21$ cents. Check: $21 \cdot 24 = 504$ ✓.

A.2 Proportions

A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal: $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}$. They turn up whenever one quantity is a fixed multiple of another: the price is a fixed multiple of the number of items, the miles are a fixed multiple of the hours at a steady speed, and the real distance is a fixed multiple of the distance on the map. If a recipe needs $3$ cups of flour for $12$ muffins, then every muffin takes the same share of flour, so $24$ muffins take twice as much and $6$ muffins take half.

The standard way to solve a proportion is to cross-multiply, and here is why it works. Start with $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}$ where $b$ and $d$ are not zero. Multiplying both sides by $b$ keeps the equation true, and gives $a = \dfrac{bc}{d}$. Multiplying both sides by $d$ then gives $ad = bc$. Cross-multiplying is nothing more than multiplying both sides by both denominators, the second legal move of Chapter 2. Because neither denominator is zero, each step can be undone, so the new equation has exactly the same solutions. It also hands you a test: $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ exactly when $ad = bc$. For $\frac{x}{12} = \frac{21}{14}$ it says $14x = 12 \cdot 21 = 252$, so $x = 18$. Reducing first is quicker: $\frac{21}{14} = \frac32$, and $12 \cdot \frac32 = 18$.

The hard part is not solving but setting up. Keep the same kind of quantity in the same place on both sides: flour over muffins on the left means flour over muffins on the right. Write the units in, as in $\dfrac{3 \text{ cups}}{12 \text{ muffins}} = \dfrac{x \text{ cups}}{30 \text{ muffins}}$. A flipped side, muffins over flour, would give a wrong answer with every step done correctly. The other route is to find the unit rate first, here $\frac{3}{12} = \frac14$ cup per muffin, and multiply by the new count. Both routes are the same equation seen two ways, so use whichever suits the numbers.

Two warnings. First, a word problem often carries numbers the question does not need. The oven temperature does not change with the size of the batch; the eggs do, but nobody asked about them. Ask of each number: if I doubled the batch, would this one double? Use the ones that do and are asked about. Second, a proportion works only when the ratio really is constant. A taxi that charges $\$3$ plus $\$2$ per mile is not proportional, because $2$ miles do not cost twice what $1$ mile does. A proportion also solves a sharing problem: when two quantities are shared $2 : 7$ and the total is $45$, there are $2 + 7 = 9$ equal parts, each $45 \div 9 = 5$.

Example 3. A proportion with a number that does not belong

A recipe makes $12$ muffins from $3$ cups of flour and $2$ eggs, and bakes them for $25$ minutes at $350$ degrees. How many cups of flour make $30$ muffins?

  1. Only the flour and the number of muffins change together. The eggs, the time and the oven are not part of this question.
  2. Set flour over muffins on both sides: $\dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{x}{30}$.
  3. Multiply both sides by $30$: $x = \dfrac{3 \cdot 30}{12} = \dfrac{90}{12} = 7.5$.
  4. Check by the unit rate: $\frac{3}{12} = \frac14$ cup per muffin, and $30 \cdot \frac14 = 7.5$ ✓. The batch needs $7.5$ cups of flour. $\blacksquare$

Solve $\dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{x}{24}$.

Answer: $x = 15$

Cross-multiplying: $8x = 5 \cdot 24 = 120$, so $x = 15$. Check: $\frac{15}{24}$ reduces to $\frac58$ ✓.

Example 4. Mixing two ratios

The ratio of apples to oranges is $3 : 4$, and the ratio of oranges to pears is $6 : 5$. What is the ratio of apples to pears?

  1. The two ratios share the oranges, but write them with different numbers, $4$ and $6$. Make them the same number. The least common multiple of $4$ and $6$ is $12$.
  2. Multiply the first ratio by $3$: $3 : 4 = 9 : 12$. Multiply the second by $2$: $6 : 5 = 12 : 10$.
  3. Now the oranges are $12$ in both, so apples : oranges : pears is $9 : 12 : 10$, and apples to pears is $9 : 10$, already in lowest terms.
  4. Check with real fruit: $12$ oranges go with $9$ apples, and $\frac{9}{12} = \frac34$ ✓; they go with $10$ pears, and $\frac{12}{10} = \frac65$ ✓. $\blacksquare$

Simply writing $3 : 5$ ignores that the "$4$" and the "$6$" count oranges on different scales. The shared quantity must be made equal before the outer two are compared.

A photo $6$ inches wide and $4$ inches tall is enlarged so that it is $15$ inches wide. If the enlargement keeps the same shape, how many inches tall is it?

Answer: $10$ inches

$\frac{6}{4} = \frac{15}{x}$, so $6x = 4 \cdot 15 = 60$ and $x = 10$. Check: the width grew by a factor of $\frac{15}{6} = 2.5$, and $4 \cdot 2.5 = 10$ ✓.

Which of these is a proportional relationship between cost and amount?

  • A taxi charges $\$3$ plus $\$2$ per mile.
  • A tutor charges $\$25$ per hour, with no other fee.
  • A phone plan costs $\$20$ plus $\$5$ per gigabyte.

Answer: the tutor

Only the tutor's cost doubles when the hours double. The taxi charges $\$5$ for $1$ mile and $\$7$ for $2$, and the phone plan $\$25$ for $1$ gigabyte and $\$30$ for $2$: each has a fixed charge on top, so the ratio of cost to amount changes with the amount.

A.3 Percent of and percent change

Percent means "per hundred", and that makes it a ratio of the kind in A.1, with the second term fixed at $100$. To say that $35\%$ of the voters back a plan is to say that the ratio of supporters to voters is $35 : 100$, which is $7 : 20$ in lowest terms, so $35\% = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35$. Nothing is new but the choice of $100$, a convenience like the unit rate: it puts every comparison on the same footing. Is $\frac{7}{20}$ a bigger share than $\frac{9}{25}$? Hard to say. Is $35\%$ bigger than $36\%$? Easy.

Every percent problem has three numbers, the whole, the part and the percent, and one fact joins them: part over whole equals percent over $100$. That is a proportion of A.2, solved the same way whichever number is missing. Multiply both sides by the whole and the part is $\frac{p}{100}$ times the whole, so $35\%$ of $60$ is $0.35 \cdot 60 = 21$, the word "of" meaning "times". Divide instead by $\frac{p}{100}$ and the whole appears: if $21$ is $35\%$ of a number, the number is $21 \div 0.35 = 60$. Divide the part by the whole and put the result per hundred, and the percent appears: $\frac{21}{60} \cdot 100 = 35$. These are three uses of one fact, so if you catch yourself memorizing three rules, go back to the proportion.

Now a price goes from $\$40$ to $\$50$. It has risen by $\$10$, but "by what percent?" asks for that change as a share of something, and the something has to be named. It is the original amount. A percent change says how big the change is compared with where you started, just as "per hundred" counts hundreds of the starting amount: $\frac{10}{40} = 25\%$. Run it backward, from $\$50$ to $\$40$, and the change is again $\$10$, but it is now measured against $\$50$: $\frac{10}{50} = 20\%$. A $25\%$ rise and a $20\%$ fall are the same change in dollars, and there is no contradiction, because the base differs. In general $\text{percent change} = \dfrac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \cdot 100$, and a negative result is a decrease. The same recipe with the true value underneath is a percent error: a scale reading $52.5$ grams for a $50$-gram standard is off by $2.5$ grams, or $\frac{2.5}{50} = 5\%$.

There is a shorter way to see a change. Raising a number $x$ by $15\%$ adds $15\%$ of it to itself, $x + 0.15x$, and taking out the common factor $x$ gives $1.15x$. So a $15\%$ increase is multiplication by $1.15$, and a $15\%$ decrease is multiplication by $1 - 0.15 = 0.85$. This is the multiplier. It joins two steps, find the percent and add it on, into one: a price with $8\%$ tax is $1.08$ times the price, and after $20\%$ off it is $0.8$ times. It also says how to undo a change: if you know $1.15x$, divide by $1.15$.

The multiplier also exposes the classic trap. A price rises $20\%$ and then falls $20\%$. Is it back where it began? Start at $\$100$. The rise makes it $1.2 \cdot 100 = \$120$. The fall takes $20\%$ of the new price, not the old, which is $\$24$, and leaves $\$96$. Together the two multipliers are $1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96$, a net fall of $4\%$. The fall comes from a bigger base than the rise did, so a rise and a fall by the same percent never cancel.

Example 5. A percent change from two values

A town had $8{,}000$ people in 2015 and $9{,}200$ in 2025. What is the percent change in its population?

  1. The change is $9{,}200 - 8{,}000 = 1{,}200$ people.
  2. The original is the base, the population in 2015: $\frac{1{,}200}{8{,}000} = 0.15$, which is $15\%$.
  3. Check with the multiplier: $8{,}000 \cdot 1.15 = 9{,}200$ ✓. The population rose $15\%$. $\blacksquare$

Dividing by $9{,}200$, the newer number, would give about $13\%$. It is the wrong base: the question asks how much the town grew compared with how big it was.

A pair of boots costs $\$45$, and the sales tax is $8\%$. How many dollars is the tax?

Answer: $\$3.60$

$8\% = 0.08$, and $0.08 \cdot 45 = 3.6$. Check: $10\%$ of $45$ is $4.5$, and $8\%$ is a little less ✓.

Example 6. Undoing a raise

After a $15\%$ raise, Dana earns $\$23$ an hour. What did she earn before the raise?

  1. The raise multiplied the old wage by $1.15$. Call the old wage $x$, so $1.15x = 23$.
  2. Divide both sides by $1.15$: $x = 23 \div 1.15 = 20$.
  3. Check by raising it: $15\%$ of $20$ is $3$, and $20 + 3 = 23$ ✓. She earned $\$20$ an hour. $\blacksquare$

Taking $15\%$ of $\$23$ and subtracting it gives $\$19.55$, which is wrong. The $15\%$ was taken from the old wage, not from the new one, so it cannot be taken off the new one.

A phone's price fell from $\$80$ to $\$68$. What was the percent decrease?

Answer: $15\%$

The change is $80 - 68 = 12$ dollars, and $\frac{12}{80} = 0.15$, which is $15\%$. Check: $80 \cdot 0.85 = 68$ ✓.

A $\$50$ shirt goes up in price by $10\%$, and then the new price goes down by $10\%$. What is the final price in dollars?

Answer: $\$49.50$

After the rise: $50 \cdot 1.1 = 55$. After the fall: $55 \cdot 0.9 = 49.5$. Not back to $\$50$, as the trap predicts: $1.1 \cdot 0.9 = 0.99$.

A.4 Repeated change

Suppose a town of $8{,}000$ people grows $5\%$ a year. After one year the multiplier of A.3 gives $8{,}000 \cdot 1.05 = 8{,}400$. In the second year the town grows another $5\%$, but of what? Of the $8{,}400$ it has now, not of the $8{,}000$ it began with. So the second year multiplies by $1.05$ again, $8{,}400 \cdot 1.05 = 8{,}820$, and a third year gives $9{,}261$. Every year multiplies by the same $1.05$, so after three years the total multiplier is $1.05 \cdot 1.05 \cdot 1.05$, written $1.05^3$ for short. The exponent says how many copies of the multiplier are multiplied together and nothing more, and a calculator will tell you that $1.05^3 = 1.157625$. Repeated change is repeated multiplication.

Compare that with simple counting. Adding $5\%$ of the original three times makes $15\%$, a multiplier of $1.15$, and $8{,}000 \cdot 1.15 = 9{,}200$. The repeated answer, $9{,}261$, is larger by $61$, and the reason is that later steps take their percent of growth that is already there: the second year's growth is $420$, which is the first year's $400$ plus $5\%$ of that $400$. A "simple" change takes every step from the original amount; a repeated change, also called a compound change, takes each step from the latest amount. Over two or three steps the gap is small, and each extra step widens it. Notice too that the total growth is $1.157625 - 1 = 0.157625$, about $15.8\%$ and not $15\%$.

A decrease works the same way with a multiplier below $1$. A car that loses $20\%$ of its value each year is multiplied by $0.8$ each year, so after three years it is worth $0.8^3 = 0.512$ of what it was, and the total loss is $1 - 0.512 = 0.488$, or $48.8\%$. It is not $60\%$, because each year's $20\%$ is taken from a smaller amount than the year before. A useful check on any answer: growth should beat simple counting, and decay should lose less than simple counting does.

The same idea handles a percent of a percent. If $40\%$ of a club are juniors and $25\%$ of the juniors are on the team, then the junior players are $0.25 \cdot 0.4 = 0.1$ of the club, which is $10\%$. Percents of percents multiply, they do not add. So do successive discounts. A coupon for $10\%$ off, used on a price already cut by $30\%$, takes $10\%$ of the smaller price. The multipliers are $0.7$ and $0.9$, the price becomes $0.63$ of the original, and the single discount that does the same is $1 - 0.63 = 37\%$, not $40\%$. Since multiplication can be done in either order, $0.7 \cdot 0.9 = 0.9 \cdot 0.7$, it makes no difference which discount comes first, and a price discounted and then taxed ends at the same place as one taxed and then discounted.

Read the last line of the question before the first number. It may ask for the final amount, for the total multiplier, for the overall percent change, which is the multiplier minus $1$, or for the single discount that matches several, which is $1$ minus the multiplier.

Example 7. Savings that earn on their earnings

A savings account holds $\$2{,}000$ and earns $10\%$ interest a year, each year's interest being added to the balance. How much is in the account after $3$ years, and how does that compare with adding $10\%$ of the original three times?

  1. Each year multiplies the balance by $1.1$.
  2. Year by year: $2{,}000 \to 2{,}200 \to 2{,}420 \to 2{,}662$.
  3. All at once: $1.1^3 = 1.331$, and $2{,}000 \cdot 1.331 = 2{,}662$ ✓. The balance is $\$2{,}662$.
  4. Simple counting adds $3 \cdot 200 = 600$ and reaches $\$2{,}600$, so repeated change is worth $\$62$ more. $\blacksquare$

The extra $\$62$ is interest earned by earlier interest, which simple counting never sees.

A town of $5{,}000$ people grows $10\%$ a year. How many people live there after $2$ years?

Answer: $6{,}050$ people

$5{,}000 \cdot 1.1 = 5{,}500$, and $5{,}500 \cdot 1.1 = 6{,}050$. Adding $10\%$ of $5{,}000$ twice would stop at $6{,}000$.

Example 8. Two discounts are not one big one

A $\$200$ jacket is marked down $30\%$, and a coupon then takes $10\%$ off the sale price. What does the jacket cost, and what single discount is the same?

  1. After the markdown: $0.7 \cdot 200 = 140$ dollars.
  2. After the coupon, taken from the sale price: $0.9 \cdot 140 = 126$ dollars.
  3. The total multiplier is $0.7 \cdot 0.9 = 0.63$, so the single discount is $1 - 0.63 = 0.37$, which is $37\%$.
  4. Check: $37\%$ of $200$ is $74$, and $200 - 74 = 126$ ✓. The jacket costs $\$126$. $\blacksquare$

Adding the discounts to get $40\%$ off would give $\$120$, which is $\$6$ too low: the coupon's $10\%$ is of $\$140$, not of $\$200$.

Of the people at a fair, $40\%$ are adults, and $30\%$ of the adults arrived by bus. What percent of all the people are adults who arrived by bus?

Answer: $12\%$

$0.3 \cdot 0.4 = 0.12$, which is $12\%$. Check with $100$ people: $40$ adults, and $30\%$ of $40$ is $12$ ✓.

A $40\%$ discount is followed by a further $25\%$ off the new price. What single percent discount gives the same final price?

Answer: $55\%$

$0.6 \cdot 0.75 = 0.45$ of the price is left, so $1 - 0.45 = 0.55$ is gone: $55\%$, not $65\%$. Check with $\$100$: $\$60$ after the first, $\$45$ after the second ✓.

Practice

Give a ratio as two numbers with a colon, like $3 : 4$, and a rate or an unknown as a single number. Ask yourself in each problem what is being compared, and which numbers belong to the question.

A class has $18$ girls and $24$ boys. What is the ratio of girls to boys, in lowest terms?

Answer: $3 : 4$

The ratio is $18 : 24$. Dividing both terms by $6$ gives $3 : 4$. Check: $3 \cdot 6 = 18$ and $4 \cdot 6 = 24$ ✓.

A bag holds $20$ marbles, of which $8$ are red and the rest are blue. What is the ratio of red marbles to all the marbles, in lowest terms?

Answer: $2 : 5$

Red to all is $8 : 20$, and dividing by $4$ gives $2 : 5$. The ratio of red to blue would be $8 : 12 = 2 : 3$, a part-to-part ratio; here the question asks for part to whole.

Of $35$ students surveyed, $21$ walk to school and the rest ride a bus. What is the ratio of bus riders to walkers, in lowest terms?

Answer: $2 : 3$

Bus riders: $35 - 21 = 14$. In the order asked the ratio is $14 : 21$, and dividing both terms by $7$ gives $2 : 3$. Writing it as $3 : 2$ puts the walkers first, which answers a different question.

A $16$-ounce jar of peanut butter costs $\$4.48$, and a $40$-ounce jar costs $\$10.00$. What is the lower price per ounce, in cents?

Answer: $25$ cents per ounce

Small jar: $448 \div 16 = 28$ cents per ounce. Large jar: $1000 \div 40 = 25$ cents per ounce. The lower price is $25$ cents, from the large jar. Check: $25 \cdot 40 = 1000$ ✓.

A train covers $270$ miles in $4.5$ hours at a steady speed. What is its speed in miles per hour?

Answer: $60$ miles per hour

$270 \div 4.5 = 60$. Check by running it forward: $60 \cdot 4.5 = 270$ miles ✓.

A cyclist rides $9$ miles in $40$ minutes at a steady speed. What is her speed in miles per hour?

Answer: $13.5$ miles per hour

$40$ minutes is $\frac{40}{60} = \frac23$ of an hour. Speed is $9 \div \frac23 = 9 \cdot \frac32 = 13.5$ miles per hour. Check: $13.5 \cdot \frac23 = 9$ miles ✓.

Solve for $x$: $\dfrac{x}{15} = \dfrac{14}{35}$.

Answer: $x = 6$

Cross-multiplying: $35x = 15 \cdot 14 = 210$, so $x = 6$. Check: $\frac{14}{35} = \frac25$ and $\frac{6}{15} = \frac25$ ✓.

Solve for $x$: $\dfrac{9}{x} = \dfrac{6}{14}$.

Answer: $x = 21$

Cross-multiplying: $6x = 9 \cdot 14 = 126$, so $x = 21$. Check: $\frac{9}{21} = \frac37$ and $\frac{6}{14} = \frac37$ ✓.

A pancake recipe serves $6$ people and uses $4$ cups of milk, $3$ eggs and $2$ teaspoons of salt. The pancakes are cooked at $375$ degrees. How many cups of milk are needed to serve $15$ people?

Answer: $10$ cups

Only milk and people are proportional here and asked about. $\frac{4}{6} = \frac{x}{15}$, so $6x = 60$ and $x = 10$. Check by the unit rate: $\frac46 = \frac23$ cup per person, and $15 \cdot \frac23 = 10$ ✓.

On a map, $3$ centimeters stand for $50$ kilometers. Two cities are $4.5$ centimeters apart on the map. How many kilometers apart are they?

Answer: $75$ kilometers

$\frac{3}{50} = \frac{4.5}{x}$, so $3x = 50 \cdot 4.5 = 225$ and $x = 75$. Check: the map distance is $\frac{4.5}{3} = 1.5$ times the scale, and $1.5 \cdot 50 = 75$ ✓.

Maria earns $\$63$ for $7$ hours of work. Last week she worked on $5$ different days, $28$ hours in all, at the same rate. How many dollars did she earn last week?

Answer: $\$252$

One hour pays $63 \div 7 = 9$ dollars, so $28$ hours pay $28 \cdot 9 = 252$ dollars. The number of days is a distractor. Check: $\frac{63}{7} = \frac{252}{28} = 9$ ✓.

The ratio of cats to dogs is $5 : 6$, and the ratio of dogs to birds is $9 : 4$. What is the ratio of cats to birds, in lowest terms?

Answer: $15 : 8$

Make the dogs $18$ in both: $5 : 6 = 15 : 18$ (multiply by $3$) and $9 : 4 = 18 : 8$ (multiply by $2$). So cats : dogs : birds is $15 : 18 : 8$, and cats to birds is $15 : 8$, which is in lowest terms. Check: $18$ dogs go with $15$ cats, and $\frac{15}{18} = \frac56$ ✓, and with $8$ birds, and $\frac{18}{8} = \frac94$ ✓.

Juice concentrate and water are mixed in the ratio $2 : 7$. How many cups of concentrate are in $45$ cups of the mixture?

Answer: $10$ cups

The mixture has $2 + 7 = 9$ equal parts, so one part is $45 \div 9 = 5$ cups and the concentrate is $2 \cdot 5 = 10$ cups. As a proportion: $\frac{2}{9} = \frac{x}{45}$, so $9x = 90$ and $x = 10$. Check: the water is $7 \cdot 5 = 35$ cups, and $10 + 35 = 45$ ✓.

A restaurant bill is $\$48$ before the tip. You leave a $15\%$ tip on the bill. How many dollars do you pay in all?

Answer: $\$55.20$

The tip is $0.15 \cdot 48 = 7.2$ dollars, and $48 + 7.2 = 55.2$. By the multiplier: a $15\%$ increase multiplies by $1.15$, and $1.15 \cdot 48 = 55.2$ ✓.

Maya made $21$ of her $28$ free throws in a game. What percent of her free throws did she make?

Answer: $75\%$

Part over whole: $\frac{21}{28} = \frac34 = 0.75$, which is $75\%$. Check: $75\%$ of $28$ is $0.75 \cdot 28 = 21$ ✓.

A school has $45$ seniors, and the seniors are $30\%$ of all its students. How many students does the school have?

Answer: $150$ students

Let the whole be $w$. Then $0.3w = 45$, so $w = 45 \div 0.3 = 150$. Check: $30\%$ of $150$ is $0.3 \cdot 150 = 45$ ✓.

A pair of shoes costs $\$80$. It is marked $25\%$ off, and then $8\%$ sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total in dollars?

Answer: $\$64.80$

The sale price is $0.75 \cdot 80 = 60$ dollars. The tax is on that: $1.08 \cdot 60 = 64.8$. Check the other way: the tax is $0.08 \cdot 60 = 4.8$, and $60 + 4.8 = 64.8$ ✓.

Attendance at a school play fell from $1{,}250$ people on Friday to $1{,}000$ on Saturday. What was the percent decrease?

Answer: $20\%$

The drop is $1{,}250 - 1{,}000 = 250$, and $\frac{250}{1{,}250} = 0.2$, which is $20\%$. Check: $1{,}250 \cdot 0.8 = 1{,}000$ ✓.

During a sale every price is reduced by $20\%$. A coat's sale price is $\$48$. What was its price before the sale, in dollars?

Answer: $\$60$

A $20\%$ discount multiplies by $0.8$, so $0.8x = 48$ and $x = 48 \div 0.8 = 60$. Check: $20\%$ of $60$ is $12$, and $60 - 12 = 48$ ✓. Adding $20\%$ of $48$ to $48$ would give $57.60$, which is wrong: the discount was a percent of the original, not of the sale price.

A laboratory scale reads $52.5$ grams for a mass that is known to be exactly $50$ grams. What is the percent error of the reading?

Answer: $5\%$

The error is $52.5 - 50 = 2.5$ grams, and $\frac{2.5}{50} = 0.05$, which is $5\%$ of the true value. Check: $5\%$ of $50$ is $2.5$ ✓.

A town has $8{,}000$ people and grows $5\%$ a year. How many people live there after $3$ years?

Answer: $9{,}261$ people

Each year multiplies by $1.05$: $8{,}000 \to 8{,}400 \to 8{,}820 \to 9{,}261$. All at once, $1.05^3 = 1.157625$ and $8{,}000 \cdot 1.157625 = 9{,}261$ ✓.

A $\$20{,}000$ car loses $20\%$ of its value each year. What is it worth after $3$ years, in dollars?

Answer: $\$10{,}240$

Each year multiplies by $0.8$: $20{,}000 \to 16{,}000 \to 12{,}800 \to 10{,}240$. All at once, $0.8^3 = 0.512$ and $0.512 \cdot 20{,}000 = 10{,}240$ ✓. It lost $48.8\%$, not $60\%$.

A store takes $30\%$ off a price, then a further $20\%$ off the reduced price. What single percent discount gives the same final price?

Answer: $44\%$

The multipliers are $0.7$ and $0.8$, and $0.7 \cdot 0.8 = 0.56$ of the price is left, so $1 - 0.56 = 0.44$ has gone. Check with $\$100$: $\$70$, then $0.8 \cdot 70 = \$56$ ✓.

A stock's price rises $20\%$ in one week and falls $25\%$ the next week. By what percent is the price lower after the two weeks than at the start?

Answer: $10\%$

The multipliers are $1.2$ and $0.75$, and $1.2 \cdot 0.75 = 0.9$, so the price is $90\%$ of the start: $10\%$ lower. Check from $\$100$: $\$120$, then $0.75 \cdot 120 = \$90$ ✓. Adding the changes would have given a $5\%$ fall, which is wrong.

Of $800$ students, $25\%$ are in the band, and $40\%$ of the band members play a brass instrument. How many students play brass in the band?

Answer: $80$ students

The band has $0.25 \cdot 800 = 200$ students, and $0.4 \cdot 200 = 80$ of them play brass. Check: percents of percents multiply, $0.25 \cdot 0.4 = 0.1$, and $10\%$ of $800$ is $80$ ✓.

Two plans start with $\$5{,}000$ at $4\%$ a year. In Plan A each year pays $4\%$ of the original $\$5{,}000$. In Plan B each year's interest is added to the balance, and the next year's $4\%$ is taken of that new balance. After $2$ years, how many dollars more does Plan B hold than Plan A?

Answer: $\$8$

Plan A: $2 \cdot 200 = 400$ dollars of interest, a balance of $5{,}400$. Plan B: after one year $5{,}200$, and $1.04 \cdot 5{,}200 = 5{,}408$. The difference is $5{,}408 - 5{,}400 = 8$ dollars, which is $4\%$ of the first year's $200$ ✓.

A backpack is priced at $\$150$ and is $20\%$ off. At the register a coupon takes a further $10\%$ off the sale price. How many dollars does the backpack cost?

Answer: $\$108$

Sale price: $0.8 \cdot 150 = 120$. With the coupon: $0.9 \cdot 120 = 108$. Check with the single multiplier: $0.8 \cdot 0.9 = 0.72$, and $0.72 \cdot 150 = 108$ ✓. It is $28\%$ off, not $30\%$.

A patient takes a $96$-milligram dose of a drug whose amount in the blood falls $50\%$ every $4$ hours. How many milligrams are left after $12$ hours?

Answer: $12$ milligrams

Twelve hours is $3$ periods of $4$ hours. Each period multiplies by $0.5$: $96 \to 48 \to 24 \to 12$. All at once, $0.5^3 = 0.125$ and $0.125 \cdot 96 = 12$ ✓.

Recap

Back to the puzzle

Look at one machine. Five machines make five widgets in five minutes, so each machine makes one widget in five minutes: its rate is one widget per five minutes, whatever the other machines do. One hundred machines each make one widget in those five minutes, which is one hundred widgets. The answer is $5$ minutes. The tempting answer, $100$ minutes, scales the time with the number of widgets; but the machines grow in number too, and the two growths cancel.

  • A ratio compares two numbers, and it is unchanged when both are multiplied or divided by the same nonzero number. Lowest terms: divide by the greatest common factor.
  • Part to part and part to whole are different ratios. Read the words after "to" and count what they count; the whole is the parts added together.
  • A rate is a ratio of different kinds of quantity. A unit rate puts the second quantity at one, and it is the only fair way to compare two prices or two speeds. Convert units before dividing, and let the units tell you which way to divide.
  • A proportion says two ratios are equal. Cross-multiplying is multiplying both sides by both denominators; $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ exactly when $ad = bc$.
  • Setting up is the hard part: the same kind of quantity in the same place on both sides, and only the numbers that grow together. A relationship with a fixed charge on top is not proportional.
  • To mix two ratios, first make the quantity they share the same number in both.
  • Percent means per hundred, a ratio with $100$ underneath. Part over whole equals percent over $100$ gives the part, the whole or the percent, whichever is missing; "of" means times.
  • A percent change is the change divided by the original, so the same dollars are a different percent up than down. A $15\%$ rise multiplies by $1.15$, a $15\%$ fall by $0.85$, and to undo a change you divide by its multiplier.
  • A rise and a fall by the same percent do not cancel: $1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96$.
  • The same percent applied again and again multiplies the multipliers: three years at $5\%$ is $1.05^3$, more than $15\%$ because later steps take a percent of growth already there. Percents of percents multiply, and so do successive discounts, so two discounts of $30\%$ and $10\%$ make $37\%$, not $40\%$.
