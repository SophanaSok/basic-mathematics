Prose sample

A puzzle to carry through the chapter

Draw any triangle on paper. Tear off its three corners and push them together. What do the three angles make?

Better still, actually do it — with two different triangles.

By the end of this chapter you can state the three properties distance obeys and use the triangle inequality, and prove the Pythagorean theorem by dissection.

We begin with an ordinary plane, its points, and its lines, and we take for granted the two facts everyone's eye already agrees with: through any two distinct points there is exactly one line, and any two distinct lines meet in at most one point. Bold, italic, a link and code.

The properties of distance
  1. Positivity. d(P,Q) ≥ 0, and d(P,Q) = 0 exactly when P = Q.
  2. Symmetry. d(P,Q) = d(Q,P).
  3. The triangle inequality. d(P,R) ≤ d(P,Q) + d(Q,R).

Example 1. Reading the triangle inequality

Two sides of a triangle measure 7 and 10. What can be said about the third side c?

  1. At the vertex opposite c: c ≤ 7 + 10 = 17.
  2. At the other two vertices: 7 ≤ 10 + c and 10 ≤ 7 + c. Three statements, not one.
  3. What does the third one give?Decide, then open

    c ≥ 3.

Why straight lines are called straight

Repeating the triangle inequality shows the straight route beats every broken one.

Watch out

√(a + b) is not √a + √b. Roots do not pass through sums.

Why it works

The parallel through one vertex carries the other two angles across.
