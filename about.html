Before you start

How to use this course

Read slowly, write constantly, and do not move on from a sentence you cannot justify. That is the whole method.

Read with a pencil

Mathematics is not readable at the speed of prose. Every time a line of algebra appears, work it out yourself on paper before reading the next line. If the site says $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, multiply it out. The habit feels slow for a week and then becomes the reason you understand anything.

When a chapter states a rule, try to see why it must hold before reading the justification. Even a failed attempt makes the explanation land, because you already know where the difficulty is.

The exercises are the course

Each chapter ends with practice problems that check themselves. A few notes on them:

  • Answers are matched forgivingly. For a number, 0.5, 1/2 and 2/4 are all accepted; spaces never matter; -3 and −3 (unicode minus) are the same.
  • When an exercise wants several numbers, separate them with commas in any order: 2,-3 is the same as -3, 2.
  • Every problem has a full worked solution, not just an answer. Open it after a genuine attempt — and open it always, even when you were right, to compare your route with the one shown.
  • Getting one wrong is information, not failure. The first wrong attempt usually gets a hint rather than the answer.

What a chapter asks you to do

A chapter is not meant to be read straight through and then tested. It keeps stopping to ask for something:

  • One step at a time. A chapter opens a piece at a time, with a Continue button under each piece. Where a piece ends in a question, answer it to go on — or skip it, and the course will remember to bring you back to that section. The switch under the chapter title turns this off and shows the whole page at once.
  • A puzzle at the top. Commit to a guess; the recap comes back to it. Guessing wrongly first is useful — it shows you what the chapter is for.
  • A warm-up of three questions from earlier chapters. They are not scored. If one will not come, its solution links to the section to reread before going on.
  • Your turn, after most worked examples: one problem of the same kind, to do before the method fades. Also not scored.
  • Examples with the later steps closed. Do each step on paper, then open it to compare. Likewise the boxes that ask you to predict a result before it is derived.
  • Section checks that are not typed: lines of an argument to put in order, blanks to fill inside an equation, several options to tick, or a figure to set so that it shows the answer. The lines can be dragged or moved with their arrow buttons.
  • Missions under each interactive figure: things to make the figure do. A star marks each one you manage, and they are counted on the contents page.

Only the practice set and the end-of-Part reviews count towards a chapter's progress ring. A second wrong answer there gets a second, more specific hint before the solution is suggested.

Order matters

Chapters are written to depend only on earlier ones. Skipping ahead mostly works inside a part, but Part III genuinely needs Part I, and the trigonometry chapter needs the geometry before it. If a chapter feels impossible, the problem is usually two chapters back.

Relation to Serge Lang's book

The course follows the topic sequence of Serge Lang's Basic Mathematics: the same four parts, the same chapters, in the same order. That sequence is the pedagogical heart of the book — it starts from the rules of arithmetic and refuses to use anything it has not established.

The writing here is independent: all explanations, figures, worked examples, and exercises were written for this site. If you own the book, the best use of this site is as a second voice — read a chapter there, then work the same ideas here, or the reverse. Nothing here reproduces the book's text or its problem sets.

XP, streaks, and what to review

Correct answers and completed missions earn XP — most for an answer that is right first time, less after a miss, least once the solution has been opened. The header shows your level, today's XP against a daily goal, and a streak of the days in a row you have done something. They are there to make a little work every day feel worth doing, which is what actually builds the skill. Every road to a right answer pays something; the combo below adds a little for accuracy, never for speed.

More useful is your progress page. The course notes how each question went — how many tries, whether a hint was needed, whether the solution was opened first — and from that lists the sections worth rereading, weakest first. A short version of the same note appears above each chapter's recap.

The game, and why it is built this way

The course plays like a game, but every rule is there to help the mathematics stick. Where the two pull apart, the mathematics wins.

  • Combo. Each answer that is right first time fills one of five pips, and a full meter adds up to double XP. A first miss on a practice problem costs two pips; working on with the hints after that costs nothing. Opening a solution after you got the answer, and reading it, earns a shield that absorbs your next miss — comparing your route with the one shown is where most of the learning happens.
  • Encounters. Each chapter's practice set is an encounter with a boss: the tempting wrong idea behind the chapter's opening puzzle. Every problem you solve wears it down, in any order. You start with three hearts and lose one for each problem you miss or open first; they decide the medal — gold, silver or bronze — and nothing else. Running out never locks anything; the course just points you at the section worth rereading.
  • Playable figures. Some questions are answered by building something in a 3D scene: three planes that share a line, a slanted solid of volume 12, a box six pyramids fill exactly. While the question is open the scene shows quantities but never says whether you are right — only Check does — so the answer has to come from thinking, not from wiggling until a light turns green.
  • The Arena is the only place with a clock, and it draws only from sections you have already solved, because speed practice helps with what you know and hurts with what you are still learning. The problems are freshly generated each time. A wrong answer costs a heart and stops the clock while you read the hint; "I don't know" costs nothing, so guessing never pays.
  • Levels and achievements come from the XP you have earned. The achievements reward study habits — getting things right first time, going back to a weak section and fixing it, reading solutions you got right, finishing a review set — and none rewards speed alone.

Play settings

The menu in the header holds three switches, remembered on this device only:

  • Calm mode turns off hearts, the combo, the boss, shake and sound, and makes the Arena untimed by default, for when you want the course without the game. Your XP, level, streak, hints and solutions are unchanged.
  • Sound is off until you turn it on.
  • 3D course map shows the contents page as a map of four regions. The list of chapters is always there beside it, and the map steps aside on its own on devices that cannot draw it smoothly.

Progress and privacy

Everything is saved in this browser's local storage: solved exercises (bm.progress.v1), missions and puzzle guesses (bm.play.v1), how each question went (bm.attempts.v1), XP per day (bm.activity.v1), your place in each chapter (bm.lesson.v1, bm.last), achievements, medals and Arena records (bm.game.v1), the combo and any unfinished Arena run (bm.run.v1), and your play settings (bm.prefs.v1). Without an account nothing is sent anywhere, so progress does not follow you to another browser, and clearing site data clears it. If storage is blocked, everything still works — it just is not remembered.

An account is optional. Signed in, the same records are copied to the course's database so that they follow you between devices, and each answer check (which question, right or wrong, which try — never what you typed) is logged so the author can see which questions are too hard and fix them. You can download or delete all of it from the account page.

The account service and its database are run by Supabase, a hosting company, and the course's author can see the stored records. You can sign in with an email address and a password or, where the account page offers it, with an account you already have at another service such as Google or GitHub. In that case you sign in on that service's own page; it passes on details such as your email address, name, username, picture address and your account id there, and it learns that you signed in to this site. The course never sees your password there. While you are signed in that way, this browser also keeps a key from that service, which signing out removes; to cut the link completely, remove this site from the connected apps in that service's settings.

Keyboard and accessibility

  • Answer fields accept Enter to check.
  • Interactive figures are operable from the keyboard: Tab to a slider or button, then arrow keys. Where a figure has points you can drag, Tab to the figure itself, move the selected point with the arrow keys, and press Space to switch to the next point. Where a figure lets you redirect a connection instead (as in Chapter 13's mapping diagram), Tab to the figure, choose an element with the arrow keys, and press Space or Enter to redirect it. Every figure also states its conclusion in text below it, so nothing is available only by dragging or clicking.
  • The 3D scenes work the same way: Tab to the scene, move the selected handle with the arrow keys (Page Up and Page Down for the third direction), press Space to select the next handle or the view itself, and Home to reset the view. Every scene can also be driven entirely from its sliders and buttons, and where 3D is not available the same scene is drawn flat with the same controls.
  • In the Arena, Enter checks and Esc pauses; the Untimed tempo removes the clock altogether.
  • The theme follows your system setting until you press the light/dark button, after which your choice is remembered.
